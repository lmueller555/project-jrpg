# Elden Vale Regional Setting

**Status: Approved regional geography, cultural foundations and opening
regional context around Eldenmere. Illustrative encounters remain story seeds.**

**Geography revised:** 2026-11-25, following the
[canonical world-map approval](../WORLD_ATLAS.md#approved-world-map).

**Opening revision, 2026-11-26:** [Chapter 1](../../game_01/CHAPTER_01.md)
owns the approved Fire Veinhold opening. The flashforward, Festival of Light
and quarry/excavation Act I are retired. The new story is not yet implemented.
The [regional overworld](../../../OVERWORLD.md) and
[detailed reserve](../../../SANCTUARY_RESERVE.md) describe retained exploration.
The bridge remains physically ruined, but the old cave emergency and quest
runtime are removed. Neither state implements the future attack chronology.

Elden Vale is Eldenmere's home region: a temperate wooded inland valley in
far-southern Merovar, between two unnamed north–south rivers. The ridges around
it are southern outliers of the Greyfold Mountains. The valley gives Game I
a home with agricultural work, practical engineering, religious service and
connections to neighbouring communities. These foundations remain relevant
after retiring the former Sanctuary quarry dispute.

Existing village facts are drawn from
[Chapter 1](../../game_01/CHAPTER_01.md) and the
[local map contract](../../../world/eldenmere/blockout/README.md). The continental context
is established in the [World Atlas](../WORLD_ATLAS.md) and
[Merovar](../continents/MEROVAR.md).

## The landscape around the village

Eldenmere remains a temperate woodland settlement with streams, sloping
terraces and cultivated ground. Northern uplands feed the village's main
brook, which descends south. The Upper Millrace is a distinct northern
woodland area. Serene's home occupies a raised northwest terrace; the mill
stands in the eastern part of the village.

At the wider scale, short wooded side valleys open into pasture,
mixed farms and a few small road settlements. Higher ground becomes conifer
woodland and open upland before the main mountain passes. The main lowland
route continues toward an unnamed neighbouring town south of Eldenmere; local
roads also serve outlying farms, quarry approaches and sanctuary properties.
The Leth districts now lie north in central Merovar, beyond the Vale's own
watershed, rather than along this immediate southern connection.

No lake, seacoast, desert or tropical forest is added to Eldenmere's immediate
surroundings. Rainfall, elevation and sheltered slopes provide the first
region's variation. Green vegetation in the current game does not establish
a season or a world calendar.

## Water and the work it supports

The two bounding rivers and village brook belong to a separate southern
watershed. They are not assigned to the central River Leth system. The exact
junction between the brook and the two bounding rivers remains open; no new
river names or extra local watercourses are introduced by this revision. The village's separate controlled millrace and
irrigation supplies retain their existing operation. Locally, the wheel's
feeding branch runs east–west; the brook follows its own north–south course.
MQ02's obstruction interrupts mill operation rather than halting the region's
whole water supply.

This makes water an everyday responsibility with several viewpoints. A
miller needs a reliable flow, a grower needs irrigation at the right time,
and a household needs clean access close to home. Local agreements
could assign maintenance duties and limited diversion periods. Their exact
rules should follow a specific scene rather than making Caelan the arbiter
of an entire watershed.

Future play spaces might include a shared washing place, a maintained ford,
a hillside drainage ditch, a grazing boundary or a small bridge under repair.
Their stories can begin with concrete work and competing needs.

## Homes, food and exchange

The existing village already supports flour milling, household gardens,
medicine deliveries, mechanical repair and a school. The surrounding
valley adds mixed farming, pasture, wool work, managed woodland and seasonal
labour. Tools, salt, some metalwork and other goods arrive along regional
trade routes. Households differ in what they grow, buy, make or exchange.

A possible local building tradition uses stone for foundations, steps and
retaining walls, with timber where it is practical. Raised storage and covered
work areas help households use sloping, wet ground. This extends the current
visual setting without replacing existing buildings.

Everyday foods include bread, vegetable and pulse stews, soft cheese,
river fish and preserved orchard fruit. Access and preference vary: a busy
craft household may buy flour while a farming household exchanges produce
for repairs. Shared meals can reveal relationships through who cooks, who
brings something, who cannot attend and who receives a portion at home.

## Serene's home, Elden Vale Sanctuary and community

**Serene's home** is the village household where Serene, Caelan and Tomas live.
Its established community role includes care, food, supplies, practical service
and religious guidance. These acts are part of ordinary village life, and
Serene has personal relationships with the people she serves. Caelan and Tomas
usually call it home; villagers may say Serene's house or Serene's.

Serene follows a [planetary spiritual tradition](../ELDEN_VALE_FAITH.md)
centered on care, remembrance and responsibility toward the planetary cycle,
rather than worship of gods. This does not assign identical beliefs to every
valley resident.

**Elden Vale Sanctuary** is a separate regional site from Serene's home.
Its marked reserve includes sanctuary buildings,
a wooded hill and buried foundations beyond the visible walls. The covenant
protects the ground beneath the reserve as well as its surface. The ridge
site now lies northeast of the village on a wooded shoulder of the eastern
Greyfold outliers, south of the northern wall and west of the eastern river.
A maintained spur from the eastern footpath reaches its regional miniature.
Its full buried layout and construction history remain open; the natural
confluence does not identify a buried planetary control mechanism.

The [faith guide](../ELDEN_VALE_FAITH.md) owns the Sanctuary's natural
confluence, guidance of the dead and living memorial care, including what is
known and unresolved. The [covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md) owns the reserve's
shared custodial responsibilities, permitted use and restricted excavation.
These obligations matter to the region's daily life without making the ground
unrestricted property of the order.

The wider valley could support small care households, travelling
caretakers and lay volunteers with varying resources. A remote household may
depend on these services more than a prosperous market settlement does.
Differences in funding, access and responsibility can matter without defining
a complete religious hierarchy yet.

Remembrance, neighbourly service and companionship remain cultural foundations.
The Festival of Light and its preparation quests are retired, rather than
retained as a recurring off-screen celebration. Any future community customs
should emerge from particular relationships and receive their own story role.
Lanterns can remain ordinary lamps; their presence in existing scenery or
historical captures does not preserve the removed festival.

## Authority, development and disagreement

Regent Iselin is an established regional authority and the younger sister of
**Queen Althea of Adamanthra**. The Adamanthran Crown governs through regional
governors and local institutions. Her exact jurisdictional borders, appointment history
and constitutional powers remain open. Elden Vale and nearby southern
communities form the immediate setting of her administration's concerns;
the revised opening does not assign her a new political jurisdiction.

The [Chapter 1 chronology](../../game_01/CHAPTER_01.md) replaces the old
relief-visit and quarry-request story. Iselin and Lucan reach Eldenmere after
their failed Fire Veinhold infiltration; Caelan and Mira's introduction occurs
while the western bridge is intact. Its destruction belongs to the subsequent
Carcovian attack, not to an earlier lightning surge. The southern connection
still leads toward an unnamed neighbouring town in southern Merovar, rather
than automatically entering the Leth districts. No new town name or attack
route is assigned here.

Road access, affordable building material, reliable mills and safe movement
between settlements can improve people's lives. The covenant also protects
sanctuary ground, including what lies beneath it. Serene's concern has a
legitimate basis even where she cannot explain every inherited boundary.

The [covenant's public evidence](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md#the-public-evidence-behind-serenes-caution)
can remain background records. It does not require a concealed Crown crew,
breach of trust, rescue or excavation authorization in the revised opening.
The Sanctuary's natural confluence remains established; its new plot role and
the cave's purpose remain open.

Valley residents should have varied positions. A quarry worker may want steady
work and stronger safety oversight. A farmer may welcome a road while
objecting to the land it crosses. A sanctuary volunteer may favour new tools
while wanting an excavation reviewed. None of these positions is a whole
culture's permanent attitude.

Future writing can draw stakes from these relationships. The people
and institutions involved remain responsible for their decisions; their
different interests should shape the consequences of interference with natural
aether circulation.

## Neighbours and travel

Local routes connect the valley to its Greyfold outliers and nearby southern
communities. The longer northward journey leads toward the central river,
Leth districts and, eventually, far-northern Adamanthra. Exact roads, passes
and the central crossing remain to be authored; the map does not require
visiting every named region. Redstone, Marris and Rookfen can be known through
goods and travellers before any visit. The old southward progression from
Elden Vale through Lower Leth to a delta is replaced.

For early story planning, use relative journeys: a local errand, a substantial
day outing, a trip requiring an overnight stop, or a longer regional route.
Exact distances and durations remain open until the playable road network and
story sequence are designed. A world atlas is not a scale conversion of the
current 480-pixel game view. Far-southern placement does not make the temperate
Vale polar or move the Sanctuary, mill or western crossing within local play.

The first four-person expedition now targets the Fire Veinhold. Its precise
location and access route remain open, as do any use of the Sanctuary cave as
a bypass, bridge repair and the later northward itinerary. The two bounding
rivers and guarded north/south land gates remain part of the region. Losing
the western bridge alone does not prove that every exit is unavailable; new
quest writing must account for those gates before claiming isolation.

## Scene foundations for future development

| Situation | Human relationships to develop | What it can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A shared footbridge needs repair | A craft worker, nearby households and a traveller | Cooperation, limited time and different ideas about who benefits |
| An upland family visits relatives | A host household and children with different memories of the valley | Regional variation through an ordinary visit |
| A delivery is delayed by road work | A carrier, a buyer and the people doing the work | The practical value and cost of development |
| A maintenance task affects two water users | People who know the equipment and households relying on it | Specific responsibilities without giving Caelan authority over the dispute |
| A visiting healer works with Serene | Caretakers and a patient with preferences of their own | Different techniques and shared commitments |

These are scene seeds for review, not additional committed quests. Caelan's
involvement should follow his relationships, practical ability and the help
people reasonably ask of him.
