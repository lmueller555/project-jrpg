# Project JRPG — Saga Bible

**Status:** Working narrative bible; only passages labeled CANON are authoritative.
**Version:** 0.1
**Source of truth:** [Foundational Lore](../../FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md). If this document conflicts with established foundational lore, foundational lore prevails until explicitly revised.

## Purpose

Plan a mature, morally complex, original multi-game JRPG narrative. The saga should grow from an intimate first-game conflict toward the deeper ancient and cosmic stakes over later installments. The number and titles of installments are undetermined. No single game is obligated to resolve the whole saga.

## Confirmed foundations (CANON)

- Aether permeates the universe, cannot be created or destroyed, and is manipulated as magicks. Its final original name is undetermined.
- An ancient civilization observed a spreading cosmic imbalance and dying worlds over millennia. The phenomenon's ultimate cause and whether it can be defeated remain unknown.
- Roughly 10,000 years before Game I, most ancients committed their life force to stasis at a temple, creating the protective planetary atmosphere.
- This protection binds a significant share of planetary energy. New modern life developed from the remaining aether over millennia and contains a fraction of individual ancient aetheric power.
- In the normal life cycle, energy released at death returns to the planet and is reabsorbed by dormant ancients; the cycle helps sustain the barrier.
- The ancients became objects of divine worship; the sacrificial temple remains a holy site.
- **Exactly four ancients** refused the sacrifice. They have spent millennia absorbing the life force associated with their dormant brethren, hoping to obtain enough power to confront the cosmic threat. This extraction destabilizes the planetary equilibrium.
- Those four serve as **behind-the-scenes antagonistic forces in Game I**, not fully disclosed villains. The first installment is a relatively limited regional story with a meaningful local conclusion.
- Modern inhabitants are distinct, valuable lives, not incomplete fragments of ancients. The story has a mature, morally complex tone.

## Central dramatic tensions (creative direction)

**Preservation versus defiance:** The sacrificial majority chose to shield life; the four dissenters seek to confront the original threat. The saga must interrogate both paths without assuming that a survivor's professed plan can succeed.

**Power versus moral worth:** Neither extraordinary ancient capacity nor modern limitations determine the worth of a life.

**Sacrifice and inheritance:** What responsibilities, if any, do living people owe the civilization that gave up its future for theirs? What rights may the surviving ancients claim without consent from those now alive?

**Faith and historical truth:** A real ancient sacrifice underlies the modern idea of gods, but belief and actual history are not identical.

## High-level saga progression (PROPOSED; not fixed)

1. **Game I — regional story.** Human decisions and regional consequences form the heart of a self-contained narrative. Subtle anomalies or agents hint at something older, without revealing the four or the cosmic truth.
2. **Later installment(s) — historical recontextualization.** The sacred site, ancient civilization, atmospheric nature, and hidden actors can gradually become subjects of investigation. The order and scale remain undecided.
3. **Eventual saga reckoning.** The methods and assumptions of the four survivors are challenged, and the origins and possible resolution of the cosmic imbalance become central. An ending, successful solution, sacrifice, and exact installment count are **not yet chosen**.

These are narrative horizons, not a committed trilogy.

## The four surviving ancients (CANON scope; details OPEN)

Exactly four survived outside stasis. Their names, identities, relationships, personalities, disguises, degree of coordination, specific methods, and individual arcs are undetermined. They should not be assumed to share every belief or to cause every wrong in modern history. Their shared historical objective does not prove they understand the threat correctly.

Do not finalize characters in this section without recording their motivations, distinct viewpoints, participation in the original disagreement, ongoing extraction method, and eventual arcs.

## The cosmic mystery (OPEN)

What changed the universe's aetheric balance? Where does energy move or become inaccessible? Does the phenomenon continue today? Can aetheric concentration defeat it? Is any of the ancients' interpretation correct? These questions are deliberately unresolved and must respect aether conservation when answered.

## Cross-game continuity rules

- Local characters, politics, and suffering must retain independent causal weight; the four ancients are not secretly responsible for everything.
- Earlier clues should support later reinterpretation, not contradict previously presented facts.
- An installment must deliver on its own central dramatic promise and resolution, even if broader questions remain.
- The storyteller's knowledge is not the same as player knowledge. See [Revelation Plan](REVELATION_PLAN.md).
- Preserve open questions without silently treating a compelling idea as canon.
- Historical or metaphysical changes require updating FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md first or in the same reviewed PR.
