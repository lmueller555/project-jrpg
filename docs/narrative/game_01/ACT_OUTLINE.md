# Game I — Act Outline: The Broken Covenant

**Status:** WORKING NARRATIVE / PROPOSED, except explicitly linked approved material.\
**Version:** 0.3\
**Scope:** Broad five-act narrative structure only; not a scene list, quest plan, or finalized world design.

## Authority and boundaries

This outline explores **The Broken Covenant** as the preferred current direction for Game I. It does **not** supersede the [Game I Story Bible](STORY_BIBLE.md), [Foundational Lore](../../FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md), [Saga Bible](../saga/SAGA_BIBLE.md), or [Revelation Plan](../saga/REVELATION_PLAN.md). Those documents' confirmed constraints remain binding:

- Game I is a **largely regional**, emotionally grounded, **self-contained** first installment of a multi-game saga.
- Its primary antagonist is a present-day person with agency and comprehensible motivations; modern societies' problems cannot all be attributed to ancient manipulation.
- Exactly **four surviving Ancients** remain behind the scenes; their full nature, number, identities, the Great Sacrifice, the atmosphere's origin, and cosmic catastrophe are **not fully revealed**.
- Aether is conserved, and magicks manipulate existing free-flowing aether; ancient mechanisms cannot generate limitless energy.
- The ending must resolve the regional primary conflict, leave meaningful consequences, and not depend on a sequel for emotional closure.

**Proposal status:** The broad five-act sequence, sanctuary mechanisms,
unapproved later events and outcomes remain provisional. Geography and cast
foundations have separate approvals. The specific
[regional surges and relief visit](REGIONAL_SURGES.md),

unimplemented later-act events.

## Logline — working proposal

When a regional ruler breaks an age-old religious prohibition to excavate beneath a sanctuary, a devastating aetheric disaster pits government against faith. A young member of the religious order, personally affected by the catastrophe, investigates what both institutions are concealing—and must stop a second, greater disaster without abandoning the people each institution claims to protect.

## Central themes — working direction

**Faith without blindness; leadership without treating the voiceless as expendable.** Institutional stewardship versus secrecy; development versus restraint; duty, trust, responsibility, and the human cost of apparently necessary choices.

## Act I — The Shattered Oath

**Approved structural progression:** Act I comprises three chapters: **Chapter 1 — Life in Eldenmere** (welcoming and predominantly peaceful; five mandatory introductory quests plus optional worldbuilding activities), **Chapter 2 — Beneath the Peace** (exploration, warnings, and growing political tensions), and **Chapter 3 — The Shattered Oath** (excavation catastrophe, its personal consequences, and the decision to investigate). The disaster occurs in Chapter 3, **not Chapter 1**. The approved Chapter 1 structure, proposed scenes, and unresolved details are documented in [CHAPTER_01.md](CHAPTER_01.md). The detailed sequencing of Chapters 2–3 remains proposed.

**Approved opening pressure:** Before the opening morning, a lightning surge
destroys Eldenmere's southern connection. Aldric arrives after aiding a couple
of other affected communities, seeking enough stone for regional repairs.
The marked ridge reserve, incomplete public hazard evidence, Serene's material
help and Aldric's disputed shallow trial cut now give their disagreement
concrete stakes. The end-MQ02 arrival scene and revised MQ03 dialogue now
present this context in the game. This small number of early incidents does not
move the major catastrophe into Chapter 1 or make every anomaly Aldric's doing.

**Function:** Establish the protagonist's lived faith, community, political tensions, and personal loss.

- The approved covenant protects the old ridge sanctuary's marked reserve,
  including buried foundations; ordinary care and surface inspection remain permitted.
- Repairs after a handful of lightning incidents strain resources. Aldric's
  relief efforts and request for quarry stone meet Serene's shared-custody duty
  and incomplete evidence of danger. Both contribute to the immediate recovery.
- The ruler authorizes excavation; a violent aetheric disturbance devastates a nearby settlement tied personally to the protagonist.
- Religious authorities call the calamity punishment for broken faith; government authorities describe an unforeseen accident.
- The protagonist finds evidence that neither explanation is complete and decides to investigate independently.

**Act break:** Faith gives way to doubt; the protagonist chooses truth-seeking over automatic obedience.

**Approved companion introduction:** Lucan Rooke, the Knight, enters during
Act I through a legitimate road-security assignment and initial escort.
His character foundation is recorded in the [companion guide](COMPANIONS.md);
the exact recruitment quest remains open.

**Not yet chosen:** Precise personal loss, remaining character biographies,
recruitment scene details, gameplay details, evidence and the emotional stakes
of leaving. Eldenmere's approved local setting and wider geography are recorded
in the [World Atlas](../../world/WORLD_ATLAS.md).

## Act II — The Divided Land

**Function:** Reveal sympathetic interests and contradictions on both sides while broadening the regional adventure.

- The party crosses prosperous and struggling communities, learning who benefits from, and who bears the cost of, the covenant.
- Religious institutions provide real services, while restrictions also impede opportunities and are used to defend entrenched power.
- Government development has delivered tangible improvements, but its leaders have suppressed serious risks.
- Demonstrations, arrests, and escalating reprisals divide the region.
- Evidence indicates a sanctuary held a working ancient device and that its activation, not mere trespass, produced the disturbance.

**Act break:** The protagonist uncovers that government-backed researchers took known risks; the ruler's motive is survival and prosperity, not senseless destruction.

**Approved companion introductions:** Rhea Sorel, the naturalist Aethermage,
enters early in Act II while investigating unusual aether behavior. Edran Hale,
the Sage, enters at the end of Act II when the party's account connects
injuries and failed protective workings he has encountered. They become major
sources of lore through their complementary observations during travel.
See the [companion guide](COMPANIONS.md).

**Not yet chosen:** Specific towns, factions, exact recruitment quests and
scenes, research records, order of revelations and the ruler's prior
relationship with the protagonist. The approved introduction windows do not
approve the remainder of this working act outline.

## Act III — The Truth Beneath the Mountain

**Function:** Midpoint recontextualization; break inherited institutional loyalties without revealing saga-level secrets.

- The party explores a concealed complex associated with the sanctuaries.
- They learn that the structures predate modern religious institutions and appear to form a linked regional network.
- The government seeks to harness the mechanisms for energy and development; religious leadership retained inherited warnings but hid them and cannot fully explain their origin.
- Neither side holds a complete understanding of the danger.
- The protagonist confronts both leaders and rejects their claims to exclusive authority over the truth.

**Act break:** The party becomes politically isolated and commits to averting a regional disaster independent of either institution.

**Revelation limit:** This is **not** proof to the player that the gods were mortal Ancients, that the atmosphere is their barrier, or that four survivors are harvesting planetary energy.

**Not yet chosen:** Exact antiquity of the mechanisms, credible limits of religious foreknowledge, specific discoveries, and consequences of defying both institutions.

## Act IV — The Fires of Conviction

**Function:** Escalate opposing convictions into irreversible decisions and visible suffering.

- Civil disorder and factional conflict intensify while aetheric instability spreads through the interconnected sites.
- The ruler's advisers advocate an activation intended to stabilize the core and secure essential energy for the population.
- The party discovers this plan risks diverting destructive effects onto politically weaker outer settlements.
- The ruler authorizes the plan knowing the tradeoff; the protagonist must seek a feasible intervention rather than merely condemn the decision.
- The activation triggers a large, escalating regional storm or comparable aetheric calamity.

**Act break:** The party enters the central complex to prevent the outer communities' destruction.

**Not yet chosen:** How the proposed stabilization works, whether anyone can reliably predict the consequences, what the protagonists must sacrifice to reach the core, and how the ruler justifies their choice.

## Act V — The Covenant Reforged

**Function:** Deliver a self-contained human-scale climax, irreversible costs, and earned resolution.

- The party confronts the ruler at the network's heart. The ruler argues that governance demands choices the protagonist has never had to make.
- A final confrontation ends the ruler's attempt to impose the cost on unconsenting communities.
- The party must still manage the dangerous aether already concentrated in the mechanism, not simply defeat a person.
- They prevent wholesale destruction by dispersing/redirection and shutting down the regional mechanism, with serious and lasting economic or infrastructural consequences.
- Both government secrecy and religious concealment face public scrutiny; the region begins renegotiating its covenant around accountability and shared stewardship.
- The protagonist does not automatically inherit the throne or solve every problem; affected communities must undertake a difficult recovery.

**Resolution:** The immediate disaster and principal political conflict are meaningfully resolved. The cost is real, the valley/region is changed, and the future is uncertain but hopeful.

**Not yet chosen:** Final encounter form, feasibility of safely redirecting conserved aether, the ruler's ultimate fate, concrete personal sacrifice, political agreement, and final character outcomes.

## Optional epilogue — a larger shadow (PROPOSED)

After the main ending has completed, an **unidentified observer** might inspect the experiment's aether transfer results or another small clue that suggests deliberate outside interest. The scene must **not** confirm the observer's ancient identity, reveal the four, or explain the cosmic mission. Whether to include it at all remains open.

## Dramatic progression summary

| Act | Plot question | Protagonist's shift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I — The Shattered Oath | What happened at the excavation? | Faith → doubt |
| II — The Divided Land | Who knew the danger, and why continue? | Doubt → suspicion |
| III — The Truth Beneath the Mountain | Can either institution be trusted with the truth? | Suspicion → disillusionment |
| IV — The Fires of Conviction | Who will be sacrificed to protect the center? | Disillusionment → resolve |
| V — The Covenant Reforged | What must be surrendered to save the region responsibly? | Resolve → earned hope |

## Risks, continuity tests, and future work

1. **Protagonist agency:** The hero must do more than uncover papers and respond to decisions made by others; define consequential choices in every act.
2. **Antagonist relationship:** Build real earned trust, indebtedness, or earlier admiration between protagonist and ruler before their ideological rupture.
3. **Religious nuance:** Include compassionate believers, institutional reformers, and legitimate sanctuary cautions; do not flatten faith into corruption.
4. **Regional mechanism logic:** Specify its aether sources, limits, conservation, and how a local shutdown can contain regional damage without conveniently restoring planetary equilibrium.
5. **World stakes:** Avoid revealing the true ancient atmospheric system during Game I; the mystery stays appropriately limited.
6. **Companion arcs:** Every principal party member should bring an independent moral perspective and a reason to travel.
7. **Earned cost:** Name a personal, irreversible price the protagonist pays. A generic damaged economy alone may not provide emotional climax.
8. **Ending credibility:** Governance reform must be a plausible beginning rather than a magical guarantee of lasting harmony.
9. **Saga linkage restraint:** Hidden Ancients can supply opportunity or observe, but the ruler and religious authorities must own their actions.
10. **Proposal/canon separation:** Do not promote the five-act plan or any detail within it to canon without explicit creative approval and corresponding documentation updates.
