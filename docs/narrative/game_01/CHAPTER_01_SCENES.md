# Game I — Chapter 1 Mandatory Quest Scene Treatment

**Status:** PROPOSED scene designs; not final scripts, canon dialogue, level design or technical requirements.  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Parent:** [Chapter 1 Structure](CHAPTER_01.md) and [Character Foundations](CHARACTERS.md)

## Opening principles

Give the player control promptly. Every required scene must change a relationship, teach how Eldenmere lives, present an actionable problem, or introduce a choice. Avoid making Caelen an errand courier for an hour. The player should enjoy a complete miniature adventure before larger political developments dominate. Act I's excavation disaster remains reserved for Chapter 3.

## MQ01 — A Day Like Any Other

**Scene 1: Morning bells.** Caelen awakens as bells ring. Player explores his modest room and sanctuary, interacts with a few personalized objects, and can speak to Tomas making an ill-advised improvement to an everyday device. Tutorial: movement and inspection without a forced exposition cutscene. *Dialogue opportunity:* Tomas asks whether Caelen has ever woken before the bells voluntarily; Caelen notes that Tomas was clearly awake to make the bell late.

**Scene 2: Serene's request.** The sanctuary is busy. Serene is coordinating help for neighbors, not waiting around to give a quest. She asks Caelen to make a short circuit with medicine, bread, and written notes. The player chooses delivery order. Serene offers one grounded observation: faith is demonstrated by what is done for a neighbor.

**Scene 3: The three households.** One family appreciates care but worries about work; an older resident shares a personal tradition for honoring the dead; a practical-minded villager appreciates the sanctuary yet questions whether remote religious leaders should set regional policy. Conversations are short, voluntary in depth, and never punish the player for asking questions. Avoid making anyone a caricature.

**Scene 4: An unexpected need.** A household requires a little more help than its parcel: perhaps lift a fallen shelf using simple magicks or coordinate with Tomas to safely transport a patient. Give the player an active, low-stakes decision. The outcome is reassuring; no sinister omen is required.

**Scene 5: Return home.** Serene follows up by name on the people helped. Tomas wants to do something bigger than small errands. A miller arrives with a practical problem, naturally leading to MQ02.

**Quest outcome:** The player knows a walkable portion of Eldenmere, understands why Caelen belongs, sees Serene care for people, and hears gentle disagreements about institutional authority. Tutorials remain unobtrusive.

## MQ02 — A Little Trouble at the Mill

**Scene 1: Miller's frustration.** Milling has stopped, threatening ordinary preparations for the coming festival. The miller thinks the problem may be mysterious aetheric interference, but Mira suspects the water channel.

**Scene 2: Reunited at the wheel.** Mira and Caelen resume a familiar argument: she trusts inspection and repairs; he cautions against assuming every old restriction is pointless. Their warmth is unmistakable. *Dialogue opportunity:* Mira says, 'If the gods wanted this wheel to turn, they could start by removing the branch.' Caelen replies, 'Perhaps they sent someone with a wrench.'

**Scene 3: Journey upstream.** The player explores a short wooded path with optional details about water management and local creatures. If combat is present, it is limited and understandable; a noncombat obstacle can serve the same narrative purpose. Mira and Caelen independently notice different clues.

**Scene 4: The obstruction.** Debris jams a control gate. A single tool cannot safely restore flow. Player uses both Mira's mechanical insight and Caelen's small-scale magicks to release pressure, clear obstruction, and prevent damage. Their collaboration is the lesson, not which ideology wins.

**Scene 5: Mill running.** The miller and workers celebrate an immediate, tangible result. Mira muses that better engineering could spare the village repeat failures; Caelen admits she has a point, while asking what happens when designs scale beyond anyone's understanding. Neither knows the upcoming catastrophe.

**Quest outcome:** A satisfying miniature adventure, paired competencies, and earned friendship.

## MQ03 — The Regent's Road

**Scene 1: Preparations.** The village anticipates Aldric's arrival. Player helps with a modest local need: clearing the route or resolving a scheduling snag. Ambient NPCs praise the ruler's tangible aid but also debate taxes, infrastructure and restrictions on sacred land.

**Scene 2: The welcome.** Aldric arrives without a triumphal villain cue. He acknowledges workers and asks after specific families. Caelen recognizes the person who came with relief supplies during a brutal winter. The player should understand why he is admired.

**Scene 3: A fair decision.** Two local groups dispute use of a road or water allocation. Aldric listens to both, asks what would let both sides keep their livelihoods, and brokers a practical compromise rather than handing down a simplistic decree. Caelen contributes a factual detail or proposes a workable option, giving player agency.

**Scene 4: Caelen and Aldric.** Aldric remembers young Caelen helping unload winter supplies and asks whether he intends to remain at the sanctuary. Caelen answers sincerely. Aldric encourages curiosity rather than mocking faith.

**Scene 5: A restrained argument.** Serene and Aldric disagree over excavation beneath protected sanctuaries. She warns that inherited prohibitions deserve caution; he asks how the region can solve growing shortages if the order refuses to explain or reconsider restrictions. Neither exposes classified lore or acts absurdly.

**Quest outcome:** Aldric earns real respect. The covenant conflict is comprehensible without assuming either institution is right.

## MQ04 — The Festival of Returning Light

**Scene 1: Preparation, not chores.** Caelen and Tomas help Serene make the square welcoming; players may visit optional activity stations. Festival belief holds that life returns to the divine after death, but dialogue presents this as local faith, not an omniscient account of cosmic mechanics.

**Scene 2: An inhabited celebration.** Elders remember absent loved ones, children race between lanterns, workers congratulate Mira on the repaired mill, and someone repeats a joke or small favor from MQ01. Ambient dialogue responds to optional activities where practical.

**Scene 3: A quiet family beat.** Serene gives Caelen a brief chance to reflect on why the village matters. Instead of a sermon, she asks about his hopes; he admits comfort in remaining near those he loves.

**Scene 4: Brothers by the river.** Tomas wants to see the world. Caelen instinctively tries to protect him by minimizing the dream; Tomas asks to be taken seriously. They reconcile imperfectly. *Illustrative dialogue:* Tomas: 'You keep telling me the valley is enough. How would you know?' Caelen: 'I know who's here.' Tomas: 'So do I. That's why I can bear to go.'

**Scene 5: Lantern ceremony.** Player can participate in a brief, reflective ritual without a gameplay penalty for choosing how Caelen responds. The occasion ends warmly.

**Scene 6: The letter.** After the festival, a messenger delivers notice authorizing government excavation near a sanctuary. Serene masks concern in front of Caelen. Once alone, she retrieves an old warning; neither she nor the player knows the true Great Sacrifice. Fade out on unease, not destruction.

**Quest outcome:** Community reaches its emotional peak, personal ambitions surface, and the final image sets up Chapter 2's tensions.

## Optional-content integration and pacing

Optional community quests may open between main scenes and remain available through the festival, subject to chronological coherence. Do not make optional tasks prerequisites for emotional comprehension. Use optional callbacks (a familiar villager recognizes help; Tomas's invention is visible), not major survival branches. Main quest estimate 60–90 minutes; side content 30–60 minutes as a flexible target, not a quota.

## Open design decisions

Precise dialogue, village layout, controls, rewards, use of combat, the three households' identities, water-channel mechanism, Aldric's adjudicated dispute, festival liturgy, Tomas's precise conflict, and the camera/player presentation of Serene's closing scene. The dialogue examples are tonal tests, not approved lines.

## Scene acceptance questions

Does each quest have its own small payoff? Are all five central people understandable before the disaster? Does Caelen act rather than only listen? Are skeptical villagers and religious believers both human? Does Aldric show admirable judgment before political disagreement? Is the festival tender without explaining the ancient cosmic secret? Can players end Chapter 1 feeling at home and a little curious about what comes next?
