# Character sheet template

**Design approval:** 2026-11-03. Individual character facts, interpretations and
scene proposals retain their own explicitly recorded approval status.

Use one authoritative sheet per character. Link to it from the character index
and relevant story guides instead of maintaining competing biographies.
[Lucan's sheet](LUCAN.md) is the first worked example.
[Mira's sheet](MIRA.md) applies the same structure to bereavement and repair,
with approved characterization and voice references distinguished from open
biography details and hypothetical scene events.

## Record conventions

- **Established:** Explicitly approved character facts or characterization.
- **Proposed:** New characterization, interpretations or scene ideas awaiting approval.
- **Open:** Not yet decided; do not fill gaps with invented history.
- **Not applicable:** A field intentionally does not apply to this character.

Record the source or approval context for established entries. Describe the
character at the opening of Game I unless another story stage is identified.
Separate intended growth from changes that have already happened. A dialogue
reference is not automatically a canonical event or an implemented game line.
Major characters use the full profile; supporting characters can retain only
the fields useful to their role.

## Relationships

For each important person, record the opening relationship, what this character
wants from them, admiration or resentment, trust, conflict, debts and intended
change. Mark each entry's status and source. Do not assume feelings are mutual.

## Voice and behavior

Record speech rhythm, vocabulary, humor, conversational habits, mannerisms,
affection, anger, apologies, behavior under pressure and behavior when safe.
Include what the writer should avoid. Attach status and source to each entry.

## Secrets, knowledge and character arc

Record concealed information and reasons for concealment, known facts, mistaken
beliefs, player-disclosure limits and timing. Keep author knowledge separate
from character knowledge and respect the [revelation plan](../../saga/REVELATION_PLAN.md).

Record the starting outlook, central contradiction, tests of values, pivotal
choices, setbacks, intended growth and possible end state. Unchosen events and
outcomes remain open, even when a direction of growth is established.

## Show, Don't Tell

Write five hypothetical scenarios that express the character through actions
or inactions rather than spoken lines. Vary the situations: competence, joy,
vulnerability, relationships, a flaw or a possible growth beat. Not every
scenario needs to illustrate grief or conflict.

Each scenario should contain:

- **Status:** Proposed for review until explicitly approved. Approval of a trait
  does not automatically approve a scene illustrating it.
- **Stage:** Opening characterization or a possible later stage; do not present
  intended growth as something already completed.
- **Setup:** An external, player-observable situation that creates a choice.
- **Observable beats:** Concrete actions, pauses, posture, object interactions
  or changes in attention. The player should be able to read the scene without
  explanatory dialogue, an internal monologue or a caption naming the emotion.
- **Inaction / trade-off:** What the character does not do, or what acting this
  way costs them. Make the choice meaningful rather than a decorative gesture.
- **Character signal:** The inference intended for the writer and reviewer,
  not text to display to the player.

Keep hypothetical triggers, objects and relationships distinct from established
history. Do not invent a canonical keepsake, injury or past event merely to
support a gesture. These are portrayal proposals, not approved quests,
animations, asset requests or gameplay changes. Use a pattern and its context
to suggest character, rather than treating one ambiguous gesture as proof.

## Dialogue and expression references

Write ten numbered examples covering contrasting expressions, not ten versions
of a catchphrase. Suggested coverage: signature confidence, reassurance, pride,
anger, fear, failure/apology, affection, humor, loyalty and growth.

### Dialogue structure

**Direction approved:** 2026-11-04.

- Give each speaker an immediate reason to speak: ask for information, make
  plans, disagree, share an observation, offer care or withhold something.
  Other speakers need concerns of their own, not just questions that set up
  the featured character's best line.
- Vary turn lengths and conversational shapes. Use sustained thoughts,
  practical exchanges, partial disclosures, ordinary invitations, unanswered
  offers, disagreements and silence.
- Avoid making keyword echoes, clever reversals, setup-and-payoff banter or
  quotable closing lines the default rhythm across the cast.
- Do not replace every joke with a leading question and a perfectly expressed
  insight. Characters need not understand or summarize their whole emotional
  pattern in order to speak about an experience.
- Retain wit when it fits the person and situation. Clear, complete sentences
  and thoughtful speeches are welcome; natural dialogue does not require
  constant stammering, filler or unfinished sentences.
- Let a conversation finish with a practical next step, a boundary, a lingering
  disagreement or shared company. Growth can appear in the response and
  subsequent action rather than a statement of the lesson learned.

Each example should contain:

- **Expression and story stage**
- **Context:** A hypothetical situation unless an actual scene is cited.
- **Dialogue:** A line, sustained thought or exchange that exposes the
  character's choices; no fixed question-and-answer format is required.
- **Acting cue:** Delivery, gesture or action that gives the line meaning.
- **Character signal:** The trait or contradiction illustrated.
- **Status / source:** Distinguish existing references from new proposals.

These are voice guides, not obligatory lines to repeat in every scene.
The overall set should demonstrate range rather than uniform cleverness.
