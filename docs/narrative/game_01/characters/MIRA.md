## Background and the meaning of repair

**Established foundation:** Mira lost both parents at 12, and Halden took her
in. Working alongside him on the mill is where she gained proficiency as a
mechanic. She threw herself into the work to bury the loss, finding enjoyment
in repairing things while not knowing how to mend the pain in her own heart.

Halden recognized her continuing grief and encouraged her to open her workshop
at 17. At the start of the game, she is 18 and finds purpose in maintaining
Eldenmere's infrastructure. That purpose and her practical independence have
not resolved the loss. The connection between bereavement and repair does not
establish a mechanical cause of her parents' deaths.

### Established age-based timeline

| Age | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 12 | Loses both parents; Halden takes her in. She develops her mechanical skills through work with him on the mill, immersing herself in it to bury her grief. |
| 17 | Opens her workshop with Halden's encouragement; he recognizes that her competence has not healed her grief. |
| 18 | Game I begins. Maintaining Eldenmere's infrastructure gives her purpose, but she still does not know how to mend the brokenness she feels within her heart. |

**Established interpretation:** A damaged mechanism offers a kind of reassurance
her grief does not: she can observe what is wrong, try something, and see a
change. Making things work gives her agency and genuine pleasure. When the
loss feels overwhelming, the same activity can become a way to avoid sitting
with it.

Her inability to "fix herself" describes her relationship to the loss, not an
objective judgment that she is defective. Her curiosity, humor, friendships
and enjoyment of craftsmanship are real; they should not all be exposed as
false appearances concealing sadness.

**Still open:** Her parents' names and occupations, whether their deaths occurred
together, the circumstances, her birthplace and life before 12, specific early
repairs, and the workshop's property and funding arrangements. Halden's care
is established, but formal adoption or guardianship procedures are not.
Do not assign an accident, aether incident, inherited workshop, parental tool
or connection to current events without approval.

## Combat progression concept

Mira is an **Engineer** whose robotics are **worn over intact limbs**. Her equipment
uses visible straps, articulated supports and accessible tools. It does not
replace any part of her body or imply an injury or surgical history.

### Wearable engineering

- Begin with her arm rig and six-unit Weapons/Protection/Support allocation.
- Unlock attachments, techniques, additional worn armor and reactor improvements.
- Let new components expand what Mira personally does in combat, with Iron Man
  as inspiration for wearable engineering.
- Preserve allocation tradeoffs; increasing capacity should not make every
  system available simultaneously without a meaningful choice.

Acquisition methods, later components and capacity milestones remain open.
The independent workshop-management robot idea concerns her workshop, not a
second controllable combatant.

### Ultimate concept: piloted mech

Mira uses her kit to suit up into a large piloted mech for **five turns**, with
Gundam as a reference for scale. Expanded power routing and enhanced versions of
her techniques could build on familiar controls. Exact actions, costs, duration
handling, acquisition and the ultimate's name remain undecided. At its conclusion
she returns to her ordinary worn equipment.

## Voice and behavior

**Existing scene foundation:** Uses concrete observations, manageable next
steps, familiar humor and practical checks on other people's safety. She can
admit difficulty without presenting herself as an infallible expert.

**Approved writing guidance:**

- Let technical language clarify what someone can observe or do; avoid jargon as a personality substitute.
- Give enthusiasm room to become a longer observation or shared activity, without requiring a clever answer about usefulness.
- Keep everyday warmth, curiosity and genuine delight. Most conversations need not mention bereavement.
- When grief surfaces, favor plain words, hesitation and an unfinished thought over an explanatory speech about her psychology.
- Let personal disclosures remain partial or end with a boundary. Listeners should not exist only to prompt a neat explanation of her grief.
- In difficult private moments, she may return to a task or find something to check. This is an approved acting reference, not a required routine animation.
- Let concern sometimes become too much practical advice, but preserve her demonstrated patience and willingness to collaborate.
- Show growing trust through her accepting company, asking for help or leaving a task unfinished for a while.
- With Caelan, begin from existing familiarity and a mostly unspoken understanding of parental loss. Do not make every interaction explain their grief; their particular shared memories are for later exploration.

**Avoid:** Treating her as a defective machine, making every invention a grief
symbol, declaring her responsible for her parents' deaths, implying joy means
she has forgotten them, or making a single repair, romance or revelation cure
her loss. Grief does not eliminate competence, humor or a future she can want.

## Show, Don't Tell

These five hypothetical scenarios express Mira without spoken lines, internal
monologues or explanatory emotion captions. They draw on established
characterization, but their particular triggers, actions and events remain
proposals for review. They do not revise existing quest scenes or commission
new animations. Later-growth scenarios do not imply that her grief is resolved.

### Scenario 1 - Work as shelter

**Status:** Proposed for review.
**Stage:** Early village life; the particular grief reminder is hypothetical.
**Setup:** At closing time, Mira sees a child outside the workshop take a
parent's hand as the family heads home.

**Observable beats:**

- She pauses with a tool halfway to its storage place, following the family
  with her eyes until they leave view.
- Instead of finishing the closing routine, she returns to a small mechanism
  that has already been tested successfully.
- It completes its cycle cleanly. She watches it, adjusts the same bracket
  again, and starts another test.

**Inaction / trade-off:** She does not put the tool away or leave, although
there is no remaining fault to repair. A bounded task takes the place of rest.

**Character signal:** Repair gives her somewhere to direct herself when grief
is harder to approach. The family is a proposed situational reminder, not a
newly established trigger or a flashback explaining her parents' deaths.

### Scenario 2 - Genuine delight in discovery

**Status:** Proposed for review.
**Stage:** Ordinary workshop life.
**Setup:** With no urgent repair waiting, a little experimental mechanism
finally completes its first clean cycle.

**Observable beats:**

- Mira follows the motion closely, then breaks into an unguarded grin.
- She beckons Tomas closer and shifts the mechanism so he can see the moving
  parts instead of keeping the successful experiment to herself.
- She lets him turn the handle, settling beside him to watch another cycle.
  Her attention stays on their shared discovery, not on whether anyone praises her.

**Inaction / trade-off:** She does not immediately turn the experiment into
a useful assignment or justify spending time on it.

**Character signal:** Curiosity and happiness are genuine parts of her life.
Making something work can be play, not merely a disguise for pain.

### Scenario 3 - Helping without taking over

**Status:** Proposed for review.
**Stage:** Early village teaching; a hypothetical workshop beat, not a rewrite of MQ04.
**Setup:** Liam struggles to balance a small frame he is assembling. Mira
could straighten it much faster herself.

**Observable beats:**

- She begins reaching for her own tool, notices Liam still trying to place
  the joint, and sets it back down.
- She steadies only the unsupported side, tilting the frame slightly so the
  imbalance becomes visible to him.
- When he changes the placement, she gradually releases her support. He
  completes the adjustment while she waits beside him.

**Inaction / trade-off:** She does not take the frame out of his hands or
replace his attempt with her own. She gives up speed and a perfectly controlled result.

**Character signal:** Her competence makes room for another person's agency.
Patient help preserves the learner's ownership of the achievement.

### Scenario 4 - Quiet care for Halden

**Status:** Proposed for review.
**Stage:** Ordinary life after opening her workshop.
**Setup:** Halden arrives after a tiring day at the mill, lowers his tool bag
and pauses before finding somewhere to sit.

**Observable beats:**

- Mira notices the pause and stops arranging the parts in front of her.
- She slides her better chair toward him, clears a place for his cup and
  takes the smaller stool herself.
- She sits with him rather than immediately opening his bag to look for work.
  For a while, the parts on her bench remain untouched.

**Inaction / trade-off:** She does not make him explain his tiredness or turn
the visit into another job. She gives up her comfortable seat and progress on her project.

**Character signal:** Practical attentiveness expresses affection for the
person who took her in. The hypothetical tired evening does not establish a
chronic illness, injury or new family event for Halden.

### Scenario 5 - Accepting company without earning it

**Status:** Proposed for review.
**Stage:** Possible later growth; exact timing remains open.
**Setup:** Caelan visits while Mira is absorbed in a harmless personal
experiment. He sets a second chair beside hers without presenting a task.

**Observable beats:**

- She reaches toward a tool, then looks between the unfinished mechanism
  and the chair he has brought.
- She sets the tool down and covers the loose parts instead of finding
  another useful job to do for him.
- She settles beside him. The experiment remains unfinished in view, and
  she lets it stay that way while they share the quiet.

**Inaction / trade-off:** She does not repay the company with a repair, or
require the project to be finished before she can stop.

**Character signal:** Receiving care can become enough in itself. This is a
possible expression of trust, not a cure for grief or a declaration of romance.
The unfinished task is nonessential; she is not abandoning an unsafe civic repair.

## Ten dialogue and expression references

All ten examples were approved as voice and expression references on 2026-11-03,
with dialogue-structure revisions approved on 2026-11-04. The revised set varies
turn lengths, practical goals, personal boundaries and unresolved feelings;
occasional dry humor remains intentional.
Examples 1 and 3 retain dialogue from existing scenes; the other examples are
approved reference sketches, not implemented quest dialogue. New contexts and
acting cues remain hypothetical and do not establish childhood events, the
cause of death or romance. Later-growth examples illustrate the approved
direction of development, not changes already completed at the opening.

### 1. Dry confidence - early village work

**Context:** Caelan anticipates Halden's relief before the mill actually works.

> **Caelan:** "Halden will be pleased."
>
> **Mira:** "Let's give him a working mill before we tell him how pleased to be."

**Acting cue:** Matter-of-fact, with a small smile; her attention returns to the problem.

**Character signal:** Understated humor and a preference for useful results over premature claims.
**Status / source:** Existing dialogue, [MQ02 mill inspection](../CH01_MQ02.md).
The additional acting cue is approved as a reference, not an implemented action.

### 2. Patient encouragement - everyday teaching

**Context:** A learner apologizes for spotting a problem she overlooked.

> "You noticed something I missed. Don't apologize for that. Show me where
> it catches. We'll start there."

**Acting cue:** Makes room beside her rather than taking the work away.

**Character signal:** Competence leaves space for another person's observation and agency.
**Status:** Approved voice reference; hypothetical scene.

### 3. Protective care - after the MQ02 fight

**Context:** Caelan tries to dismiss the need for an injury check.

> **Mira:** "It wasn't easy. Are you hurt?"
>
> **Caelan:** "I'll manage."
>
> **Mira:** "Let me look first."

**Acting cue:** Checks his hands and shoulders before discussing their next task.

**Character signal:** Practical care and honest acknowledgment of difficulty, not invulnerability.
**Status / source:** Existing dialogue and injury-check action,
[MQ02 aftermath](../CH01_MQ02.md).

### 4. Genuine delight - discovery for its own sake

**Context:** A small experimental mechanism finally moves as intended, with
Tomas nearby to share the discovery.

> **Mira:** "Look - the arm's clearing it now. It kept catching there. I nearly
> took the whole thing apart again."

*She turns the handle, smiling, then shifts aside.*

> **Mira:** "Tomas, come and try it."

**Acting cue:** Stays absorbed in the moving parts and makes room for Tomas,
without waiting for praise or a question about the invention's purpose.

**Character signal:** Pleasure emerges through attention and sharing the
experience. Invention can be play, not merely a cover for grief.
**Status:** Approved voice reference; hypothetical scene.

### 5. Anger at careless risk - a value under pressure

**Context:** Someone dismisses another person's concern about an unsafe design.

> "They asked whether it was safe. You told them they were worrying too much.
> That's not a test. If you don't know, say you don't know, and let us check."

**Acting cue:** Firm and plain; she stops the work before arguing further.

**Character signal:** Her anger targets disregard for people and evidence rather than injured pride.
**Status:** Approved voice reference; no specific accident is established.

### 6. Grief behind repair - a private conversation

**Context:** Someone she trusts asks why she keeps working when she is upset.
The disclosure scene and listener remain unchosen.

> **Mira:** "I was twelve when they died. Halden took me in. I started helping
> him at the mill. There was always work to do."

*She puts the part down.*

> **Mira:** "I don't really want to talk about the rest tonight."

**Acting cue:** Leaves the part on the bench and lets the boundary stand,
rather than offering a complete explanation or immediately resuming the task.

**Character signal:** A concrete recollection connects work and loss without
requiring her to explain her own psychology. Trust can include choosing how
much to disclose; the death circumstances remain unassigned.
**Status:** Approved expression of the established background; disclosure scene remains open.

### 7. Fear of another loss - a vulnerability

**Context:** A companion notices she is checking an already-tested device again.

> "I know. We checked it."
>
> "Then what are you looking for?"
>
> "Something I missed. Something I could still put right before anyone gets
> hurt. Give me a moment. I'm finding it hard to leave it alone."

**Acting cue:** Eventually lets the companion help with the check rather than silently repeating it.

**Character signal:** Fear can complicate her practical competence without making her incapable.
**Status:** Approved voice reference; this does not imply a device caused her parents' deaths.

### 8. Apology for trying to solve a feeling - possible growth beat

**Context:** A friend wants to be heard, but she has interrupted with tasks
and suggestions.

> **Mira:** "Sorry. I interrupted you."

*She takes her hands away from the work.*

> **Mira:** "You were telling me what happened. Go on."

**Acting cue:** Gives the friend time to continue without adding another
suggestion or asking them to acknowledge her apology.

**Character signal:** A specific apology and changed attention demonstrate
listening without a speech about her tendency to fix things.
**Status:** Approved voice reference; the scene and timing remain open.

### 9. Affection through accepting company - developing trust

**Context:** Caelan visits and asks whether she needs help.

> **Caelan:** "Need a hand?"
>
> **Mira:** "No. I'm stopping for today."

*She sets the tool aside.*

> **Mira:** "Are you staying for a bit? You can pull that chair over."
>
> **Caelan:** "For a while."

**Acting cue:** Makes room for him and leaves the task alone, without turning
the invitation into another job.

**Character signal:** An ordinary request for company expresses trust without
a joke about being broken or a lesson about usefulness.
**Status:** Approved voice reference; does not establish romance or an actual visit.

### 10. Hope without erasing loss - possible later growth

**Context:** After an enjoyable day with a companion, she remembers her parents.
The activity, listener and later story point remain unchosen.

> **Mira:** "I had a good time today."

*She stays beside the companion.*

> **Mira:** "I kept wanting to tell my parents about it."

*She is quiet for a while.*

> **Mira:** "I'd like to do this again. Would you?"

**Acting cue:** Allows the memory and the silence without forcing a smile or
explaining away the feeling. Her last question asks about a shared future,
not whether the listener approves her grief.

**Character signal:** Missing her parents and wanting another good day can
coexist without a concluding statement about healing.
**Status:** Approved voice and growth reference, not a fixed ending.
