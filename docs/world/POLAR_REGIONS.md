# Auratia's Polar Regions

**Status: Approved geographic and cultural foundations. Illustrative encounters
remain story seeds; exact borders, distances and routes remain open.**

Auratia's poles have different geographies. The **Nivren Crown** is a northern
circumpolar region centred on an ice-covered ocean, including the far northern
shores of Merovar and Serevin. **Istrava** is a separate southern continent,
with land beneath its ice, beyond Othara across the **Southern Ocean**.

These additions preserve the existing placement and climates of Merovar,
Serevin, Othara and the Rillan Isles. Othara remains south of the Amber Sea,
with its dry northwest, elevated Ossan Plateau and wet eastern Velin Raincoast.
Open ocean separates it from Istrava. See the [World Atlas](WORLD_ATLAS.md)
for the whole-world arrangement.

| Polar region | Geographic character | Broad settlement pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nivren Crown** | Northern polar ocean, drifting sea ice, continental fjords, tundra and offshore islands | Long-established coastal communities, mobile herding households and seasonal crews; no permanent towns on the drifting central pack |
| **Istrava** | Southern polar landmass, interior ice plateau, glacier coasts and volcanic island fringe | Small island communities with generations of residence; rotating mainland shore stations; deep interior sparsely surveyed |

Both regions experience long summer daylight and long winter darkness toward
their respective poles. Their seasons occur at opposite times of year. No
calendar dates or precise polar-circle latitudes are established here.

## Nivren Crown

The Crown extends around the northern polar ocean rather than forming a new
northern continent. Its southern margins meet Merovar and Serevin's already
cold northern country. Forests give way to tundra before the exposed polar
coasts. The name describes a broad geographic region, not a single state or
people.

### Pale Sea

The **Pale Sea** contains the North Pole. A shifting cover of sea ice lies over
deep water; pressure ridges, open leads and drifting floes change the surface
from one season or voyage to another. Coastal summer openings can permit
careful navigation, while the central pack remains much harder to reach.

A route marked safe in an earlier account may no longer exist. Navigators
compare observations rather than treating one traveller's chart as permanent.
Temporary camps and ice crossings require supplies and a planned return;
there are no established permanent towns on the drifting central ice.
The map must distinguish this sea ice from continental land.

### Rimeward Shores

The **Rimeward Shores** include the far northern fjords, tundra, coastal islands
and adjoining settlements of Merovar and Serevin. Sheltered valleys and lower
slopes have more vegetation than the exposed coast. Northern forest districts
provide timber; farther out, soil, fuel and dependable landing places are much
scarcer. Inland districts outside this coastal description remain available
for later development.

Coastal fishing, boatbuilding and cold-water trade support permanent households.
Mobile herding households follow seasonal pastures and maintain relationships
with settled villages. A harbour owner, a hired deckhand, a herder and a family
with a small root garden need different things from the same summer market.
Neither coastal residence nor seasonal travel defines a person's ancestry or
political allegiance.

Timber houses are practical near forests. More exposed settlements favour
thick stone walls and sheltered courtyards, with space for drying equipment
and working out of the wind. Smoked fish, preserved roots and imported grain
feature in everyday meals. Access to boats, storage space and reliable trade
creates substantial differences between neighbouring households.

### Skeld Isles

The **Skeld Isles** are rocky offshore islands within the northern region.
Cliffs, narrow anchorages, exposed slopes and cold-water channels shape their
settlements. Some islands support fishing harbours; others serve as seasonal
landfalls or places to shelter and repair a boat. The group is separate from
the warm central-sea Rillan archipelago.

Island households maintain nets, boats, stores and sheltered workspaces.
Timber and grain must often arrive by sea. A delayed vessel can interrupt
ordinary teaching, cooking and repair work as well as commercial voyages.
Residents include fishers, builders, traders, teachers, carers and people who
would prefer a livelihood less dependent on the next arrival.

### Culture, exchange and disagreement

Winter music, carved storytelling panels and teaching bring people together
when travel is difficult. The panels can record a household story, a remembered
journey or a comic local mishap; they are not a universal sacred script.
Summer markets make room for marriages, visits and communal repairs alongside
trade. Individual settlements and families vary in how publicly they mark
these occasions.

Some ports support professional rescue crews. Elsewhere, travellers use shared
caches and are expected to replenish what they take. Neither arrangement is
universal. Commercial ships can benefit from local observations, shelter and
rescue work while leaving little payment behind. Disagreements over rescue
funding can therefore matter as much as an exceptionally dangerous voyage.

Northern households trade fish, boat work and knowledge of coastal passage,
while importing grain, tools, cloth and timber where local supplies are
insufficient. Trade connects them to the wider economies of Merovar and
Serevin without making every household a merchant or every coast prosperous.

**Possible encounter:** visitors help carry supplies to a depleted rescue
cache before a change in weather. They can hear why residents disagree about
the visiting fleet's contributions without being appointed to settle the
region's commercial rules.

## Istrava

**Istrava** is Auratia's fourth major continent. The Southern Ocean separates
it from Othara and the other inhabited trade routes around the Amber Sea.
Its rocky landmass supports a broad interior ice sheet, with glaciers flowing
toward the coast. The continent is not a southern extension of Othara or a
single floating sheet of sea ice.

Its harsh environment limits travel and settlement. It does not make every
resident an explorer, every visitor a hero or every community an isolated
keeper of hidden knowledge.

### White Expanse

The **White Expanse** is the high interior ice plateau containing the South
Pole. It is a polar desert: little precipitation falls, but the cold allows
ice to accumulate over long periods. Wind, severe temperatures and concealed
crevasses make apparent stretches of level ground dangerous to cross.

The deep interior is sparsely surveyed. Shore observations and occasional
journey accounts do not amount to a complete chart of inland routes. There
are no established large inland cities, reliable overland trade corridors or
easy passages across the pole. Features shown in the classroom map's interior
are broad physical geography rather than a promise of playable destinations.

### Bluewall Coast

Along the **Bluewall Coast**, glaciers reach the sea between rocky headlands
and occasional usable landings. Ice cliffs can calve into coastal waters.
The few shore stations occupy suitable exposed rock and sheltered ground,
rather than being permanent buildings on drifting ice.

Mainland stations host rotating navigators, healers, surveyors and repair crews.
Cooking, cleaning, keeping stores, tending illness and maintaining equipment
are essential parts of their life together. Weather journals and supply
records receive careful attention because a later crew may depend on them.
A poorly recorded departure can create a practical danger for people who
have never met the writer.

The name describes a glacier coast, not a continuous wall around the entire
continent. Specific harbours, station names and safe seasons remain for later
story planning.

### Cinder Isles

The **Cinder Isles** form a subpolar volcanic fringe off Istrava. Dark rock,
cold seas, patches of hardy vegetation and local hot springs distinguish them
from the deep ice plateau. Geothermal warmth is limited to particular sites;
it does not heat the whole island chain or create a hidden tropical country.

Some small fishing households have lived here for generations. Their homes
are not all temporary expedition camps. Boat repair, net work, household
crafts, care and teaching continue between the arrivals of seasonal crews.
Fisheries support food and exchange, but imported grain, timber and equipment
remain important.

Bright doors, patterned household textiles and communal kitchens bring colour
and company to exposed settlements. Some households enjoy hosting newcomers;
others need quiet or resent the demands of a visiting crew. Long residence
does not give all families equal access to boats, good buildings or imported
supplies.

### Culture, exchange and disagreement

Visitors encounter a mixture of island families, returning seasonal workers
and first-time arrivals. Practical knowledge passes through teaching, weather
journals and supply records, as well as conversation. The cook who notices a
damp store or the repairer who finds a failing hinge can protect a household
as effectively as a navigator choosing a safe approach.

Supplies reach the region through long sea voyages, including connections
from Othara. The Southern Ocean passage is a separate undertaking from ordinary
shipping within the Amber Sea. Freight charges and delayed deliveries can
threaten food and fuel even when a distant merchant considers the route
profitable. Island residents and rotating station crews may disagree over
which stores should be reserved for the next winter.

**Possible encounter:** a late delivery arrives with damaged packing. Visitors
help dry and sort the cargo while residents identify what can be shared and
what must be kept for an isolated shore station. Their useful involvement
precedes any wider disagreement over prices or responsibility.

## Maps, magicks and story boundaries

The world map should show northern pack ice over water and Istrava's southern
ice over a distinct landmass, with open ocean between Istrava and Othara.
Eldenmere, the Greyfold rain shadow, the Leth watershed, the warm central sea
and all fifteen previously established regional foundations retain their
approved relationships. The six polar subregions extend that regional
framework; they do not replace it.

Coastlines, seasonal ice limits and station coverage are broad depictions.
Political borders, precise distances, named shipping companies, settlement
populations and the extent of unvisited interiors remain open. The map does
not establish a calendar or a route that is safe throughout the year.

Magicks obey the same conservation limits here as elsewhere. Small uses can
assist skilled work without eliminating the need for food, fuel, shelter or
careful navigation. Neither polar latitude nor ancestry automatically grants
exceptional magical power.

No Ancient homeland, original sacrificial temple, survivor identity or
companion birthplace is assigned to either pole. Local religious beliefs
remain beliefs and do not settle the world's hidden cosmology. Game I's
regional story does not require a polar expedition. See
[Foundational Lore](../FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md) and the
[Revelation Plan](../narrative/saga/REVELATION_PLAN.md).
