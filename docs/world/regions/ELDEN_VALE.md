# Elden Vale Regional Setting

**Status: Approved regional geography, cultural foundations and opening
regional context around Eldenmere. Illustrative encounters remain story seeds.**

**Implementation:** The [first regional overworld](../../OVERWORLD.md) now
represents the boundary rivers, woodland, foothills, fields, guarded walls and
lightning-destroyed western road bridge. Aldric's [arrival cinematic](../../REGENT_ARRIVAL.md)
and MQ03 dialogue present the relief visit and covenant disagreement.

Elden Vale is Eldenmere's home region: a wooded inland valley on
the western foothills of Merovar's Greyfold Mountains. The valley gives Game I
a home with agricultural work, practical engineering, religious service and
connections to neighbouring communities. It also gives the covenant dispute
specific land, livelihoods and relationships at stake.

Existing village facts are drawn from
[Chapter 1](../../narrative/game_01/CHAPTER_01.md) and the
[local map contract](../eldenmere/blockout/README.md). The continental context
is established in the [World Atlas](../WORLD_ATLAS.md) and
[Merovar](../continents/MEROVAR.md).

## The landscape around the village

Eldenmere remains a temperate woodland settlement with streams, sloping
terraces and cultivated ground. Northern uplands feed the village's main
brook, which descends south. The Upper Millrace is a distinct northern
woodland area. The sanctuary occupies a raised northwest terrace; the mill
stands in the eastern part of the village.

At the wider scale, short wooded side valleys open into pasture,
mixed farms and a few small road settlements. Higher ground becomes conifer
woodland and open upland before the main mountain passes. The main lowland
route continues south toward the upper Leth market towns; local roads also
serve outlying farms, quarry approaches and sanctuary properties.

No lake, seacoast, desert or tropical forest is added to Eldenmere's immediate
surroundings. Rainfall, elevation and sheltered slopes provide the first
region's variation. Green vegetation in the current game does not establish
the festival's date or a world calendar.

## Water and the work it supports

The larger watershed connects the village brook through downstream
tributaries to the River Leth. The village's separate controlled millrace and
irrigation supplies retain their existing operation. Locally, the wheel's
feeding branch runs east–west; the brook follows its own north–south course.
MQ02's obstruction interrupts mill operation rather than halting the region's
whole water supply.

This makes water an everyday responsibility with several viewpoints. A
miller needs a reliable flow, a grower needs irrigation at the right time,
and a household needs clean access close to home. Local agreements
could assign maintenance duties and limited diversion periods. Their exact
rules should follow a specific scene rather than making Caelan the arbiter
of an entire watershed.

Future play spaces might include a shared washing place, a maintained ford,
a hillside drainage ditch, a grazing boundary or a small bridge under repair.
Their stories can begin with concrete work and competing needs.

## Homes, food and exchange

The existing village already supports flour milling, household gardens,
medicine deliveries, mechanical repair and a school. The surrounding
valley adds mixed farming, pasture, wool work, managed woodland and seasonal
labour. Tools, salt, some metalwork and other goods arrive through the upper
Leth routes. Households differ in what they grow, buy, make or exchange.

A possible local building tradition uses stone for foundations, steps and
retaining walls, with timber where it is practical. Raised storage and covered
work areas help households use sloping, wet ground. This extends the current
visual setting without replacing existing buildings.

Everyday foods include bread, vegetable and pulse stews, soft cheese,
river fish and preserved orchard fruit. Access and preference vary: a busy
craft household may buy flour while a farming household exchanges produce
for repairs. Shared meals can reveal relationships through who cooks, who
brings something, who cannot attend and who receives a portion at home.

## Sanctuary and community

The sanctuary's established role includes care, food, supplies, practical
service and religious guidance. It is part of ordinary village life. Serene
has personal relationships with the people she serves, and its household
includes Caelan and Tomas.

The **old ridge sanctuary near Eldenmere** is a separate site from this
inhabited village sanctuary. Its marked reserve includes sanctuary buildings,
a wooded hill and buried foundations beyond the visible walls. The covenant
protects the ground beneath the reserve as well as its surface. The ridge
site's exact position and full buried layout remain open; it is not
established as the original sacrificial temple.

Civil authorities, sanctuary keepers and the community share a custodial
trust in the reserve. Protection sits alongside duties of care and practical
service; it does not make the ground the order's unrestricted property or
prevent ordinary passage and maintenance within established limits. New
quarrying and disturbance of buried works require joint review and exceptional
authorization. See the [Sanctuary Covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md).

The wider valley could support small sanctuary households, travelling
caretakers and lay volunteers with varying resources. A remote household may
depend on these services more than a prosperous market settlement does.
Differences in funding, access and responsibility can matter without defining
a complete religious hierarchy yet.

The Festival of Light is established as a gathering of remembrance and living
company. Elira's lantern recalls her husband; Liam chooses a purpose for his
own. Lanterns remain ordinary lamps. Additional valley customs, such as
bringing a homebound neighbour a portion of food or helping repair an older
lantern, should emerge from particular relationships. No universal obligation
to carry a stranger's lantern is created.

## Authority, development and disagreement

Regent Aldric is an established regional authority. His exact jurisdictional
borders and the source of his regency remain open. Elden Vale and nearby
upper Leth road and quarry districts form the immediate setting of his
administration's concerns.

Before Game I opens, an extraordinarily powerful lightning surge destroyed
Eldenmere's road south to the next town. The southern connection is part of
the [classroom world map's route context](../../WORLD_MAP.md); the town has no
approved name. A handful of similar incidents have affected other parts of
the region, and Aldric has already visited a couple of communities to provide
relief. These are serious local emergencies, not a widespread cataclysm.
Their deeper mechanism is unknown to the people involved. The
[Regional Surges guide](../../narrative/game_01/REGIONAL_SURGES.md) governs
these incidents and the opening arrival context.

Repairs across the affected communities strain stone supplies, hauling and
other repair resources. Aldric seeks a nearer stone seam within the ridge
sanctuary's protected boundary to supply several towns. More distant stone
remains an alternative, with a longer haul competing for carts, money and
time needed by other repairs.

Road access, affordable building material, reliable mills and safe movement
between settlements can improve people's lives. The covenant also protects
sanctuary ground, including what lies beneath it. Serene's concern has a
legitimate basis even where she cannot explain every inherited boundary.

Serene has shared an old foundation plan and an account of workers injured
by an unexplained discharge after opening a passage. That small historical
event is distinct from the recent lightning surges; no common cause is
established. The records support caution without revealing the site's full
history or proving that any excavation would cause a catastrophe. Serene
offers existing courtyard stone, crew meals, surface inspection and help
obtaining records. This contribution defers her own repairs but cannot meet
the region's full material need. The [MQ03 discussion](../../narrative/game_01/CH01_MQ03.md)
leaves excavation permission unresolved.

Valley residents should have varied positions. A quarry worker may want steady
work and stronger safety oversight. A farmer may welcome a road while
objecting to the land it crosses. A sanctuary volunteer may favour new tools
while wanting an excavation reviewed. None of these positions is a whole
culture's permanent attitude.

The future conflict can draw its stakes from these relationships. The modern
people involved retain agency; an ancient influence does not explain every
decision they make.

## Neighbours and travel

The valley connects north and east to Greyfold upland routes, south
to the upper and Lower Leth riverlands, and through a more demanding mountain
crossing to the Redstone rain-shadow country. The coast and delta lie farther
downstream. They can be known through goods and travellers before any visit.

For early story planning, use relative journeys: a local errand, a substantial
day outing, a trip requiring an overnight stop, or a longer regional route.
Exact distances and durations remain open until the playable road network and
story sequence are designed. A world atlas is not a scale conversion of the
current 480-pixel game view.

## Scene foundations for future development

| Situation | Human relationships to develop | What it can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A shared footbridge needs repair | A craft worker, nearby households and a traveller | Cooperation, limited time and different ideas about who benefits |
| An upland family arrives for the festival | Relatives, a host household and children with different memories | Regional variation within a familiar celebration |
| A delivery is delayed by road work | A carrier, a buyer and the people doing the work | The practical value and cost of development |
| A maintenance task affects two water users | People who know the equipment and households relying on it | Specific responsibilities without giving Caelan authority over the dispute |
| A visiting healer works with Serene | Caretakers and a patient with preferences of their own | Different techniques and shared commitments |

These are scene seeds for review, not additional committed quests. Caelan's
involvement should follow his relationships, practical ability and the help
people reasonably ask of him.
