## Regional identity and emotional intention

> Elden Vale is an inhabited emerald valley beneath the immense, pale antlers
> of a long-dead creature, where rain sometimes rises from the meadows into the
> forest canopy.

Eldenmere is a warm, familiar home within an extraordinary landscape. The
Sanctuary occupies the region's quieter, older heights. Farming, practical
engineering, care, remembrance and ordinary company give both places meaning.

Develop wonder through familiar life. Caelan knows this country; he need not
react as a tourist or explain everything to the player. Let working routines,
children's play, affection and small inconveniences express how residents live
with the remarkable. Grandeur belongs alongside comfort, humor and intimacy.

The distant antler silhouette, Rising Rain and the approach from cultivated
low ground into woodland and limestone heights create the region's identity.
Keep these connected to inhabited places without requiring every local detail
to be part of one supernatural explanation. The damaged bridge adds a recent,
concrete interruption to a region with a much longer history.

## Fixed geography and travel boundaries

Elden Vale remains a temperate wooded inland valley on the western foothills of
Merovar's Greyfold Mountains. Northern woodland gives way to cultivated southern
ground; rocky wooded shoulders rise in the east. No new lake, coast, desert or
tropical biome is introduced. Green vegetation does not fix a festival date or
world calendar.

Preserve these geographic and story constraints:

- Two winding, **unnamed rivers** bound the region on the east and west.
- Northern and southern stone walls span between the riverbanks, with guarded
  openings. Opening-story restrictions remain in force.
- The destroyed **western bridge lies inside the walls**. Its far-bank road
  continues south toward the unnamed next town. Its position and its southern
  destination are compatible facts. There is no bridge over the eastern river.
- Gate release and bridge repair remain independent story facts. New ruins,
  overhead antlers, paths or weather must not create an early way around them.
- Iselin's relief caravan uses the southern land checkpoint, not the broken
  crossing.
- Eldenmere's brook and controlled millrace are separate from the boundary rivers.
  The brook descends south; the feeding millrace branch retains its east–west
  operation. MQ02 concerns the mill, not the entire watershed.
- The Sanctuary stays northeast of Eldenmere, south of the northern wall and west
  of the eastern river, reached from the eastern foothill route.
- Serene's home remains the northwest village household shared with Caelan and
  Tomas. It is separate from the regional Sanctuary; the village mill stays east.

Existing village facts come from [Chapter 1](../../narrative/game_01/CHAPTER_01.md)
and its local layout/scene contracts. Detailed town, reserve and regional-map
scales have different presentation purposes; real-world distances and exact
revised regional coordinates remain open until layout work.

river can be glimpsed below wooded rock shelves. These differences support the
same inland valley and do not change watershed identities.

## The Pale Antlers

### Form and gradual recognition

Enormous branching antlers project from the northeastern limestone ridge above
the woodland. Weathered pale surfaces resemble stone at a distance. Moss,
nesting birds and small trees occupy sheltered hollows. Their visible aging
and surrounding life make the remains part of the landscape.

Do not place the Pale Antlers, a shadow or a replacement silhouette inside
Eldenmere. The regional road toward the Sanctuary progressively reveals them. A later viewpoint exposes part
of a colossal skull embedded in the hillside: the player recognizes that the
forms belonged to something alive.

Most of the body stays buried. Exact species, age, cause of death, burial history
and any relationship to the confluence remain unresolved. Size does not establish
a deity, an ancient civilization, surviving consciousness or power-bearing bones.

The landmark should be recognizable on the regional map, in distant scenery and
in approach views. Keep one coherent arrangement and orientation across scales.
Use birds, mature trees, buildings and walking distances to communicate scale;
do not enlarge people or fill every nearby screen with bone.

### Mandatory pathing and ground contact

> **The Pale Antlers are a landscape-scale landmark with elevated branches and
> ground contacts anchored in the northeastern ridge. Their placement must
> preserve intended walkable ground, route widths, entrances, quest approaches
> and exploration loops. Collision belongs only to visible ground-contact
> structures. Overhead artwork must retain character and interaction readability.**

Design the landmark around circulation from the outset. Embed the skull and
rooted bases in rocky, already inaccessible ridge ground. Raise the main beams
above the treetops before they branch outward. Elevated spans may cross woodland
or paths, leaving the ground beneath open.

- Preserve existing usable ground and generous main-route clearances. Do not
  narrow paths, intersections, door approaches, work stations or story staging
  areas to accommodate the new silhouette.
- Only actual physical supports at ground level may have solid footprints.
  Never use the full sprite rectangle, shadow, canopy or antler silhouette as a
  collision mask. Never place invisible walls beneath an open span.
- Solid-looking bone at accessible ground must have a matching physical footprint;
  the player must not walk through visible supports.
- Put fallen fragments and smaller projections beside routes. Keep alternatives
  around scenic viewpoints. A passage beneath an antler should feel spacious,
  without becoming a mandatory narrow obstacle or navigation puzzle.
- Do not add regional exits, cross the boundary rivers or bypass the walls,
  guarded gates, broken bridge or the cave's later-game descent restrictions.
- Preserve the current player footprint and companion spacing. Do not shrink
  collision probes, relocate the player or use teleportation to hide a bad fit.

### Layering and visibility

| Component | Placement and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| **Distant silhouette** | Establishes grandeur above the woodland; no ground collision. |
| **Skull and rooted bases** | Embedded in the ridge, with visible physical supports and matching local collision. |
| **Elevated beams and branches** | Leave ground beneath traversable; use appropriate background/foreground depth. |
| **Ground fragments** | Clearly visible matching footprints outside protected circulation and staging areas. |

Leave substantial gaps between branches and keep thick beams away from important
interaction sightlines. Where an overhead foreground piece would hide Caelan,
companions or a necessary interaction, use a restrained local fade or cutaway as
needed. Retain enough outline and visible context to communicate that the branch
is overhead. The specific rendering technique belongs to implementation; an
opaque wall of artwork over a usable route is not acceptable.

Show the whole arrangement at regional scale and appropriate portions in the
detailed reserve. Preserve the same landmark, orientation and relationships
rather than creating unrelated shapes for each scene.

### Layout before finished artwork

Produce a simple plan marking routes, solid supports, overhead spans, entrances,
quest/NPC stations, viewpoints and important sightlines before commissioning
finished landmark art. Shape the antlers around that plan.

Validate ordinary walking, running, corners, party movement and return journeys
through every affected route, including beneath spans and during quest scenes.
Review visible support versus collision, camera framing and foreground occlusion
in actual gameplay. Recheck save/Continue positions and reduced-motion visibility.
The grandeur must survive these checks without restricting exploration.

## Rising Rain

Rising Rain is a longstanding local phenomenon, distinct from the recent
extraordinary lightning destruction. Its ultimate cause and any link to the
remains, ruins or confluence are unresolved. No relation to the Aethersphere is
established.

During selected quiet intervals after rainfall, droplets gather on grass,
fences and leaves, detach and drift upward. It begins near the ground. Across
meadows, thousands of droplets catch the light, then form loose curtains passing
into the woodland canopy. Higher on the approach, the rising moisture becomes
long veils moving around the Pale Antlers.

| Phase | Intended experience |
| --- | --- |
| **Signs** | Familiar preparations and changes in nearby bird activity indicate the conditions. Let residents recognize what is coming. |
| **Arrival** | A few droplets detach from grass, leaves and fences; the phenomenon becomes legible close to the player. |
| **Peak** | Upward-moving curtains catch the light across open meadows and thread into the canopy; higher mist reveals and conceals the antlers. |
| **Departure/aftermath** | The curtains thin, ordinary activity resumes and quiet wet surfaces remain. Ordinary weather separates occurrences. |

The first regional outing should offer a reliable authored opportunity to witness
it. Do not make the region's defining experience depend on an unlikely random
roll or an extended wait. Exact recurrence, timing, triggers, intensity and weather
system architecture remain to be designed; no dynamic weather system is implied
by this document.

Planned expressions of everyday adaptation include farmers bringing washing
under cover, children catching droplets beneath broad leaves, orchard workers
using collection cloths, travelers watching from covered resting places and birds
leaving exposed branches shortly beforehand. These are scene opportunities, not
new item rewards, mandatory chores or committed NPC schedules.

Keep routes, people and interaction cues readable through the effect. Weather
must not block movement, conceal an exit or grant unplanned combat modifiers,
water resources or quest progress. Respect pause/focus and reduced-motion
presentation when implemented.

## Old causeway and inhabited history

Give history a present tense. Surviving causeway sections may still support
travel; an old parapet can form part of a sheep enclosure, a surviving arch can
shelter a produce stand, and a dry stretch of paving can become a children's
play space. Guides may know that the road once continued somewhere unused now.

Near the Sanctuary, the same sense of age becomes quieter and less domestic:
buried foundations, partially exposed retaining walls and stonework disappearing
beneath roots. Suggest successive construction and repair without attributing
all works to one civilization or declaring their original purposes.

Keep the causeway inside the region. It does not repair or replace the western
crossing or introduce an eastern crossing. Its reuse should connect inhabited
places and optional discoveries without forcing new detours or erasing existing
working areas.

## Sanctuary approach and local character

Move from cultivated ground into woodland. Views of the antlers disappear, then
small memorial markers, tended plants and worn resting places establish long
keeping. The trees open onto a limestone shoulder, revealing the Sanctuary
beneath the enormous branching silhouette. Rising moisture moves through the
scene; birds occupy the high branches and the eastern river can be heard below.

From the forecourt, offer different scales of experience: inhabited hall and
cloister, intimate living memorial grove, practical relief room, surveyed seam
and darker crystal-framed cave approach. Preserve the pale limestone, slate,
emerald woodland, silver-green memorial growth and cyan/amethyst/opal crystals
with restrained amber accents. The ridge crystals and memorial tree retain
local importance beneath the larger regional landmark. The basin and carved
inlays carry related materials into quieter spaces. Windows and lamps remain
steady; atmosphere must preserve readable human activity and circulation.

The Sanctuary remains a community institution deliberately sited at a natural
confluence. Neither the Pale Antlers nor Rising Rain replaces its established
purpose or proves an explanation for the site. A full movement/occlusion review
must include the arrival party, care scene, cave rail operation and return to
free exploration.

## Folklore, observation and creator-established truth

The accepted folklore direction includes stories that a great creature lay down
to shelter the valley, or that the antlers were once a forest that learned to
walk. These are local stories, not confirmed origins. Different people can tell
different versions or disagree about their significance.

Children may call Rising Rain “the meadow giving the sky its water back.”
Farmers may have a less romantic expression because it interrupts work. A local
remembrance saying can be “Keep a place beneath the branches,” appearing in a
song, carved bench or conversation with different personal meanings. Exact
wording and placements remain scene-writing decisions.

| Distinction | Application here |
| --- | --- |
| **Designed observable features** | Enormous exposed antlers and part of a skull; weathered remains supporting living growth; droplets rising into the canopy. The remains are integrated; weather awaits implementation. |
| **Local beliefs and stories** | Shelter, a walking forest, the sky receiving water and the saying about keeping a place. They do not prove supernatural mechanisms. |
| **Unresolved creator-level questions** | Species, age, death, the oldest ruins' builders/purposes, the phenomenon's mechanism and relationships between those features. |

Do not imply souls inhabit the antlers, the creature created the confluence, or
the Sanctuary is the original sacrificial temple. Keep present-day residents'
agency and differing beliefs. Familiarity, care and practical expertise can
communicate wonder without an explanatory speech.

## Intended first regional impression

After a shower, Caelan steps out from Eldenmere toward the fields. A woman folds
damp cloth beside a cart; two children crouch in the grass. More of the meadow
comes into view as droplets lift around them. Beyond the fields, pale branching
shapes emerge through mist above the woodland. A child catches a droplet beneath
a leaf, an adult calls the children back to work, and Caelan continues on his way.

This is home to him. Preserve that familiar tone while giving the player time
to recognize the extraordinary. This is a scene brief, not a new required quest,
finished cutscene script or instruction to duplicate the existing farm actors.

## Retained regional and cultural foundations

The following established context survives the visual redesign. Statements marked
possible or future remain scene seeds rather than additional committed quests,
institutions or customs.

## Water and the work it supports

The larger watershed connects the village brook through downstream
tributaries to the River Leth. The village's separate controlled millrace and
irrigation supplies retain their existing operation. Locally, the wheel's
feeding branch runs east–west; the brook follows its own north–south course.
MQ02's obstruction interrupts mill operation rather than halting the region's
whole water supply.

This makes water an everyday responsibility with several viewpoints. A
miller needs a reliable flow, a grower needs irrigation at the right time,
and a household needs clean access close to home. Local agreements
could assign maintenance duties and limited diversion periods. Their exact
rules should follow a specific scene rather than making Caelan the arbiter
of an entire watershed.

Future play spaces might include a shared washing place, a maintained ford,
a hillside drainage ditch, a grazing boundary or a small bridge under repair.
Their stories can begin with concrete work and competing needs. Such internal
watercourse examples do not authorize new crossings of the boundary rivers.

## Homes, food and exchange

The existing village already supports flour milling, household gardens,
medicine deliveries, mechanical repair and a school. The surrounding
valley adds mixed farming, pasture, wool work, managed woodland and seasonal
labour. Tools, salt, some metalwork and other goods arrive through the upper
Leth routes. Households differ in what they grow, buy, make or exchange.

A possible local building tradition uses stone for foundations, steps and
retaining walls, with timber where it is practical. Raised storage and covered
work areas help households use sloping, wet ground. This extends the current
visual setting without replacing existing buildings.

Everyday foods include bread, vegetable and pulse stews, soft cheese,
river fish and preserved orchard fruit. Access and preference vary: a busy
craft household may buy flour while a farming household exchanges produce
for repairs. Shared meals can reveal relationships through who cooks, who
brings something, who cannot attend and who receives a portion at home.

## Serene's home, Elden Vale Sanctuary and community

**Serene's home** is the village household where Serene, Caelan and Tomas live.
Its established community role includes care, food, supplies, practical service
and religious guidance. These acts are part of ordinary village life, and
Serene has personal relationships with the people she serves. Caelan and Tomas
usually call it home; villagers may say Serene's house or Serene's.

Serene follows a [planetary spiritual tradition](../ELDEN_VALE_FAITH.md)
centered on care, remembrance and responsibility toward the planetary cycle,
rather than worship of gods. This does not assign identical beliefs to every
valley resident.

**Elden Vale Sanctuary** is a separate regional site from Serene's home.
Its marked reserve includes sanctuary buildings,
a wooded hill and buried foundations beyond the visible walls. The covenant
protects the ground beneath the reserve as well as its surface. The ridge
site now lies northeast of the village on a wooded shoulder of the eastern
Greyfold foothills, south of the northern wall and west of the eastern river.
A maintained spur from the eastern footpath reaches its regional miniature.
Its full buried layout remains open; it is not established as the original
sacrificial temple.

The ridge sanctuary was deliberately built near a real natural aether
confluence to guide the region's dead back to the planet and remember them.
Its caretakers perform a critical societal responsibility that mining would
disrupt. A living memorial grove forms part of the community's continuing care.
Serene and the keepers understand this function. The confluence belongs to the
ordinary planetary cycle, separate from the Aethersphere; its exact workings,
the buried structures' purpose and their relationship remain unresolved.

Greater regions elsewhere in Auratia also have their own sanctuaries at natural
aetherial confluences. Elden Vale Sanctuary provides the local introduction to
that wider pattern. Exploring other sanctuaries may become a recurring main-story
thread; their individual histories, routes and discoveries remain to be designed.

Civil authorities, sanctuary keepers and the community share a custodial
trust in the reserve. Protection sits alongside duties of care and practical
service; it does not make the ground the order's unrestricted property or
prevent ordinary passage and maintenance within established limits. New
quarrying and disturbance of buried works require joint review and exceptional
authorization. See the [Sanctuary Covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md).

The wider valley could support small care households, travelling
caretakers and lay volunteers with varying resources. A remote household may
depend on these services more than a prosperous market settlement does.
Differences in funding, access and responsibility can matter without defining
a complete religious hierarchy yet.

The Festival of Light is established as a gathering of remembrance and living
company. Elira's lantern recalls her husband; Liam chooses a purpose for his
own. Lanterns remain ordinary lamps. Additional valley customs, such as
bringing a homebound neighbour a portion of food or helping repair an older
lantern, should emerge from particular relationships. No universal obligation
to carry a stranger's lantern is created.

## Authority, development and disagreement

Regent Iselin is an established regional authority and the younger sister of
her northern kingdom's reigning queen. That Crown governs through regional governors
and local institutions. Her exact jurisdictional borders, appointment history
and constitutional powers remain open. Elden Vale and nearby
upper Leth road and quarry districts form the immediate setting of her
administration's concerns.

Before Game I opens, an extraordinarily powerful lightning surge destroyed
Eldenmere's road south to the next town. The southern connection is part of
the [classroom world map's route context](../../WORLD_MAP.md); the town has no
approved name. A handful of similar incidents have affected other parts of
the region, and Iselin has already visited a couple of communities to provide
relief. These are serious local emergencies, not a widespread cataclysm.
Their deeper mechanism is unknown to the people involved. The
[Regional Surges guide](../../narrative/game_01/REGIONAL_SURGES.md) governs
these incidents and the opening arrival context.

Repairs across the affected communities strain stone supplies, hauling and
other repair resources. Iselin seeks a nearer stone seam within the ridge
sanctuary's protected boundary to supply several towns. More distant stone
remains an alternative, with a longer haul competing for carts, money and
time needed by other repairs.

Road access, affordable building material, reliable mills and safe movement
between settlements can improve people's lives. The covenant also protects
sanctuary ground, including what lies beneath it. Serene's concern has a
legitimate basis even where she cannot explain every inherited boundary.

The old foundation plan and historical passage-injury account remain public
background evidence, separate from the recent bridge incident. Caretakers
have heard growling inside the cave since that incident and have kept the
entrance closed. The [Echo Chamber rupture](../../SANCTUARY_CAVE.md#echo-chamber-rupture)
is established as a consequence of the surge that caused the lightning storm and
bridge destruction: it disturbed the natural confluence and ruptured the floor
before the crew attack. This does not establish the creature's origin or a common
mechanism for the historical passage injury, the attack, the Pale Antlers or
Rising Rain. Deeper descent remains later-game content, with no current route.

Before her discussion with Serene, Iselin secretly sent a Crown crew to secure
the working area and prepare the mining operation. A panicked survivor's
report of a cave attack exposes that deployment during
[MQ03](../../narrative/game_01/CH01_MQ03.md). Serene recognizes her bad faith;
Lucan, who was not told about the crew, is taken aback. Iselin's genuine relief
work does not erase her concealed attempt to impose control over the site.

Serene stays to care for the survivor with Tomas and the driver helping.
Caelan collects Mira and travels to the reserve with her, Iselin, Lucan and
the page. The implemented route includes the Sanctuary arrival, cave rescue,
Vespermaw fight, healing-pool evacuation and return resolution. Iselin must
account for her concealed deployment, and relief arrangements address the
immediate recovery needs. No agreement to excavate is granted by this emergency
response or by completing MQ03.

Valley residents should have varied positions. A quarry worker may want steady
work and stronger safety oversight. A farmer may welcome a road while
objecting to the land it crosses. A sanctuary volunteer may favour new tools
while wanting an excavation reviewed. None of these positions is a whole
culture's permanent attitude.

The future conflict can draw its stakes from these relationships. The modern
people involved retain agency; an ancient influence does not explain every
decision they make.

## Neighbours and travel

The valley connects north and east to Greyfold upland routes, south
to the upper and Lower Leth riverlands, and through a more demanding mountain
crossing to the Redstone rain-shadow country. The coast and delta lie farther
downstream. They can be known through goods and travellers before any visit.

For early story planning, use relative journeys: a local errand, a substantial
day outing, a trip requiring an overnight stop, or a longer regional route.
Exact distances and durations remain open until the playable road network and
story sequence are designed. A world atlas is not a scale conversion of the
current 480-pixel game view.

## Design review and implementation boundary

Before art production, complete the regional/zone brief from the
[central standard](../../WORLD_DESIGN_STANDARD.md#13-required-design-brief), using
this document for approved identity, emotion, geography, inhabitants, signature
features, weather, history, folklore, exploration, sensory identity and return
experiences. Record precise layout, event triggers, assets and scope for the
actual implementation pass. Do not promote open mechanisms or scene seeds to
canon while filling the brief.

Review the first arrival, ordinary route use, the antlers' progressive reveal,
close viewpoints, the Sanctuary arrival and quiet everyday life. Check all six
regional areas as one connected landscape. Additional detail must support large
readable forms and intended travel.

Acceptance for a future redesign includes:

- All fixed geography, gate/bridge story restrictions and live village/millrace
  relationships remain coherent; existing links and saved journeys are accounted for.
- The Pale Antlers read at distant, approaching and local scales without impeding
  intended movement or hiding required characters and interactions.
- Main routes, entrances, work/NPC stations, quest staging and exploration loops
  retain clearance for the current player and travelling party.
- Rising Rain has a reliable first experience and everyday local responses,
  with ordinary weather and quieter scenes providing rhythm.
- The causeway has plausible current uses, with no new boundary crossing or
  presumed ancient-site identity.
- Observation, folklore and established truth remain distinct; local lives keep
  significance beyond the main plot.
- Actual movement, collision/occlusion, relevant story scenes, map presentation,
  pause/focus, reduced motion and save/Continue are reviewed before delivery.

story progression. It does not activate weather. Current
