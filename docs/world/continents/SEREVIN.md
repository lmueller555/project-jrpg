# Serevin

**Status: Approved continental geography and cultural foundations. Illustrative
encounters remain story seeds; exact political borders and institutions remain open.**

Serevin is a geographic label covering many communities and jurisdictions. Its
inhabitants do not share a single government, language or way of life.

Serevin lies east of Merovar across the Amber Sea in Auratia.
Its northern interior has severe continental winters. Central uplands
limit the rain reaching that interior, while moisture-bearing summer winds
feed the warmer southeastern rivers. The southwestern Namar Coast faces the
Amber Sea and connects inland roads with shipping routes. These relationships
establish travel and climate patterns; exact borders and distances remain open.

The far northern coasts and offshore islands join the Nivren Crown's
[Rimeward Shores](../POLAR_REGIONS.md). This circumpolar coastal geography
does not replace Talven's inland steppe or Vashen's warmer southeastern basin.

## Talven Steppe

Talven is a northern expanse of grassland, sheltered river valleys and dry
foothills. Winters make hay stores and dependable wells important public
concerns. Seasonal herders share the region with grain-growing villages,
quarry settlements and market towns. Wealthy livestock owners, hired drovers
and families cultivating small plots have different interests in the same land.

Permanent homes use thick earthen walls and enclosed yards; travelling camps
use portable shelters and windbreaks. Barley broth, preserved curds and roasted
roots make practical winter meals. Some households offer a newcomer a hot
ladleful before asking their business. Wayside caretakers keep lamps beside
lists of overdue travellers as an expression of concern and remembrance.

Route-songs carry accounts of journeys, family meetings and memorable disputes.
Performers earn praise for remembering another person's version fairly before
adding their own. Town scribes also collect these accounts in books, and their
choice of whose story to preserve can be contentious. A successful drover may
command more respect in one gathering than a distant official with a fine title.

Pasture councils negotiate seasonal access, while town magistrates administer
markets and contracts. New fences around a well can favour settled landowners
and obstruct an older grazing route. A possible encounter follows a delayed
grain wagon: a repair crew needs help restoring access before suspicion turns
neighbours against the newly arrived caravan workers.

## Vashen Basin

Vashen's broad southeastern rivers receive seasonal rain from the uplands.
Floodplains support rice, beans, orchards and reed beds, while higher ground
holds towns and roads. Granaries stand above ordinary flood levels; brick
homes often share shaded courtyards. Boat crews and riverside farmers work
alongside clerks, potters and families who have moved from drier districts.

Meals commonly combine steamed grain with bean stews, pickled vegetables or
fish, varying with household income and access to the river. Guests may be
given a dry sleeping platform before a formal welcome. Some neighbourhoods
hold remembrance gatherings after communal embankment repairs, sharing food
and speaking the names of former workers.

Cloth dyeing, papermaking and painted tile work connect household craft with
public life. Reading rooms and courtyard performances make room for recited
histories, comedies and instruction. A prominent local convention asks a formal
speaker to state the account they are disputing before giving their answer;
younger speakers sometimes resent the time and polish this demands. Wealthy
households can buy the education that makes the convention easier to navigate.

Water districts maintain gates and publish maintenance duties, but upstream
landowners can exert more influence than downstream tenants. A new diversion
might improve orchard yields while leaving a poorer district's washing steps
and wells unusable. An encounter could involve helping residents carry clean
water during repairs and hearing conflicting accounts of who approved them.

## Namar Coast

Namar comprises southwestern headlands, estuaries and sheltered ports facing
the Amber Sea. Coastal scrub and terraced orchards alternate with busy river
mouths. Inland cart roads bring Talven wool and Vashen produce to warehouses;
ships carry them toward Merovar, Othara and the Rillan Isles.

Stone lower storeys protect stored goods from damp, while covered balconies
provide shade for household work. Flatbreads, citrus preserves and grilled
fish share market stalls with dishes introduced by migrant families. Lodging
houses advertise prices and meal times on boards, although family-run inns
often rely on a shared table and introductions. Harbour shrines can offer
quiet rooms for prayer, news and help locating missing passengers.

Small playhouses thrive on harbour comedies and topical songs. Translators,
performers and mixed-language households continually change the local speech;
public notices may appear in several scripts. Established merchants sometimes
prefer a prestigious inland register, while dockside audiences reward a speaker
who can make themselves understood. These differences can appear in a joke or
a family conversation before they become a political argument.

Ports have their own charter councils, customs officers and working
associations. Merchant households, dock labourers and resident foreign crews
can disagree over whose needs a harbour improvement should serve. Raising
berthing fees to fund a seawall may protect valuable warehouses while driving
small fishing boats to an exposed landing. A possible encounter begins with
unloading those boats safely during a storm, before the policy dispute reaches
its next public hearing.

## Connections and story use

Serevin exchanges wool, cloth, paper, ceramics, dyes and preserved produce for Merovaran
metalwork and tools, Otharan fibres and resins, and Rillan transport services.
Seasonal contracts, marriages, study and displaced households carry customs
along these routes. Imported cloth, visiting healers and sailors' accounts can
make Serevin present in Game I while its principal story remains regional.

Modern practices here remain subject to the [Foundational Lore](../../FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md).
This atlas places no original sacrificial temple or Ancient homeland. See the
