## Fantastic landscapes that change Merovar

These are regional proposals, not ordinary places with magick added
afterward. For each one, ask four questions: **What can people count
on seeing? Who can live or travel there because of it? What past
choice left a trace? What incompatible choices remain now?** If the
history and conflict would survive unchanged without the wonder, the
geography isn’t doing enough.

Separate the observable rule from the cause. [Foundational Lore](../../FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md)
distinguishes a natural phenomenon from deliberate magicks using
available free-flowing aether. Aether is conserved and ordinary
magicks do not tap the Aethersphere. These drafts use existing snow,
water, or stone without deciding whether magicks can create matter.
Nothing here explains the Ancients or the opening’s
[recent surges](../../narrative/game_01/REGIONAL_SURGES.md). None specifies
a map change, playable area or required journey.

Craft carried forward from the actual
[map-making transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pjLSv4WNUtY)
and Hello Future Me’s
[hard](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iMJQb5bGu_g) and
[soft worldbuilding](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZVrnfniQiS8) transcripts:
make crossings and physical constraints consequential, make rules clear
where they govern choices, and leave deeper causes open where mystery
serves the place.

### What each wonder tests

Keep the central story choice different even when histories include
loss or inheritance. Hollow Crest asks whether people can consent on
behalf of those displaced; Cliff Lakes asks who can act in an emergency.
Nightglass concerns restitution and what uncertain evidence can justify.
Reedwake asks who can speak for a place that no longer occupies one
location. The Folding Orchards turn participation into a race against dawn.
Surfwood ties who can hear to who must care for the woods. The Emberways
make a private choice to leave a household consequential for people who stay.

## Elden Vale: what the confluence asks of the living

Elden Vale already has its defining wonder. The
[confluence and Sanctuary](../ELDEN_VALE_FAITH.md) are not a proposal
for another magical landscape: the Sanctuary really serves the dead,
and its [covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md) keeps protected ground
answerable to that responsibility. The exact workings and what
persists of a person remain open.

**Writer question:** Can people inherit responsibility for sacred ground
without inheriting a say in its care? Some established households might
argue that keeping this place takes obligations that survive any
resident’s death or departure. Newer residents can agree to do the
work and still object to being heard only as helpers under decisions
other families inherit. Neither disputes the Sanctuary’s work or
threatens to withhold it. Put the conflict in who counts as a keeper,
not in a second quarry negotiation or a new doctrine. No authority,
office, rite or scene is added by asking the question.

## Greyfold Uplands: the Hollow Crest

**Working name; not canon.** Above one gorge on Greyfold’s wet western
slopes, winter snow gathers into the shape of a ridge with no mountain
under it. Cornices hang over open air. Through a blue tunnel in the
snow, a traveller can see pine trees on the far side. Each spring the
white ridge thins from one end. A curtain of meltwater advances across
the gorge as the suspended snow lets go.

Only existing snowfall collects. The crest has a finite capacity, and
water that leaves it falls normally. Its form looks like a missing
mountain, but that is not evidence one stood there. People can know
the thaw’s usual order without knowing what holds the snow. This is
a longstanding phenomenon proposed for one gorge, not an explanation
of the recent surges.

**How it changed Greyfold:** In one proposed older thaw, leaders
tried to keep their only dependable crossing usable by turning part
of the falling water away from it. They kept the crossing, but the
water took away the ledge where several households lived. Those
households survived and settled elsewhere. The crossing keepers
remember the year the valley stayed connected. The displaced
families’ descendants remember being made to pay for that connection.

Descendants want the old diversion closed before it takes their
remaining grazing ground. Households across the gorge want it kept
open long enough for people to reach winter shelter and family.
The conflict is not about finding more water or making it disappear.
The places people can safely inhabit change with where their spring
water goes. No one has established a spell that can hold up the
whole crest or remove the need to choose.

**How a claim could reach Eldenmere:** A seasonal orchard worker
receives an offer for her family’s claim to the vanished ledge. The
people still keeping the crossing want a clear claim to the surviving
ground before their children build there. The money would let her
rent a home and stop following harvest work. But the offer calls her
grandmother’s departure voluntary, and the account she grew up with
describes someone carried away while protesting.

She asks Caelan to read the offer aloud twice, once without the
payment. Refusing would preserve a claim, not bring back a house.
Accepting could make another family safer under words she can’t
accept. This is a proposed encounter about consent and inheritance,
not a legal rule, new visit, quest or change to MQ03.

## Redstone March: the Cliff Lakes

**Working name; not canon.** In one part of the Redstone rain shadow,
sheets of water stand upright against native red cliffs. From a distance
they look like narrow blue blades with bands of green at their feet.
From below, a traveller can see a caravan reflected overhead against
dry stone.

A continuous band of rock holds existing water. Draw water out and
the sheet visibly shrinks; it returns only if water flows in. Sever a
loaded band and the water drops. A quarried block cannot do the same
thing elsewhere. The rule would make some dependable water pockets
visibly fantastic without ending the rain shadow or creating limitless
water. The sustaining cause is open.

**How it changed Redstone:** During a proposed older fire in the
timber workshops of a quarry, lake-tenders deliberately released a
Cliff Lake. The water stopped the fire reaching the dwellings above
the workings, but drowned people sheltering below. Some relatives say
rescuers heard answering blows. Tending families remember the fire
already reaching the outer houses. The order saved people and killed
people; whether there was time for another rescue remains disputed.

Quarry households now want a representative to share authority over
any deliberate release. Lake-tenders argue that waiting for two
decisions could cost the people a release would protect. Several
families belong to both groups. At the dry cliff, sockets from old
draw ladders survive above the stone. People hang ordinary cups from
them, upright for those saved or turned down for those lost. A
household can hang both. That memorial does not settle who decides
next time.

**What the memorial can’t pass on:** A tending household offers
its granddaughter the apprenticeship once promised to her mother.
Her mother’s quarry kin will support it only if she promises not to
authorize a release alone. Her grandfather says lives were saved
because somebody acted before everyone agreed. The girl can hang
both cups and still lose the trust of half her family, whichever
condition she accepts. If she refuses the apprenticeship, a practical
skill the community needs may stop passing between generations.
This is a proposed consequence of one family’s history, not a general
qualification for water-tending across Redstone.

## Merovar’s Rimeward Shores: the Nightglass Escarpments

**Working name; not canon.** Through the season without sunrise,
the intact wall of one [Rimeward fjord](../POLAR_REGIONS.md#rimeward-shores)
turns clear enough to see mineral veins and the outlines of caverns
sealed within it. A lamp can reveal a crack no one can reach. The
stone is still cold, heavy and solid. A piece broken off turns
opaque; cutting it into windows will not let anyone take the wonder
home. Daylight closes the view into the cliffs, not the caverns
themselves. The coast and the inland kingdom remain separate; the
effect’s cause and exact location are open.

**How it changed the coast:** During one proposed older winter,
surveyors saw an immense hollow under a terrace everyone had thought
solid. Several families left to stay with relatives and handed over
their keys so builders could inspect. By the time daylight returned,
their hosts had called the homes abandoned and divided them up. No
one ever established whether the hollow put the buildings in danger.

Some descendants mark their own doors with a second keyhole cut into
a piece of dark wood: a door they own beside a door they lost. Others
think the custom accuses children of something their grandparents did.
The displaced families now want recognition and the return of surviving
homes. Current residents can accept an independent survey and still
object to a remedy that would leave their children without homes.
Seeing into a mountain never told anyone how to divide the land above it.

## Rookfen: the Reedwake

**Working name; not canon.** An islet with reed beds, trees and
occupied houses creeps upstream while barges pass it going downriver.
Behind it, dark water opens where farmland stood. This is not a
floating island: the roots and silt move along the submerged bed.

In one proposed delta reach, on an ebb after the natural tidal salt
front has completely encircled an intact islet, the islet moves
toward freshwater. It stops where fresh water breaks the ring or
its roots reach shallower ground. Freshwater-only ground does not
move. Detached roots and poured salt do not reproduce the phenomenon.
The rule makes one kind of access unreliable even when nothing floods:
the landing can be safe and dry while the people it served have moved.

**How it divided a town:** In one proposed summer, an inhabited
islet began to move during a play performed across the channel.
Some households went ashore before it pulled away. Others stayed
deliberately, taking the players with them. They no longer wanted
to stage victories for families who had prospered at their expense.
Neither group accepted that the other had become a different town.
The old stage still faces an empty channel. The balcony that used
to answer it now stands upstream among living trees.

The two groups want to finish the play. One wants the defeated to
have speaking parts; the other demands the original ending first.
An actor welcome on both banks refuses either version. Her grandmother
was the supposed villain, and nobody has asked what she did. Which
performance counts as the town’s story helps decide who can claim
to speak for the town now. Nothing here sets its actual name, location,
a new water route, or a quest.

## Lower Leth: the Folding Orchards

**Working name; not canon.** At dusk in one belt of
[Lower Leth](MEROVAR.md#lower-leth) orchard country, rooted living wood
bends like heavy cloth. From a hill, people watch whole crowns lower
into green vaults above the meadows, fruit still hanging underneath.
At sunrise the wood stiffens in whichever shape it has reached.
A severed limb loses the effect. A mature shelter takes years to
grow; nobody can buy it and carry it away.

**How it changed public life:** Generations ago, several households
left a patron’s lockable stone hall for gatherings under living
roofs. They gained a meeting nobody had a door key for, but depended
on growers keeping the oldest trees alive. Before dawn, they open
the roof crown by crown. Someone who sleeps or works beneath each
tree gets a chance to speak before its crown is lifted. If the
gathering runs late, a grower risks leaving a fruiting tree in its
gathering shape for the day.

A grower wants to replace exhausted trees after sunrise. Seasonal
workers want to train other trees into shelter first; otherwise they
lose the place from which they have been heard. A custom devised
to take power from a hall owner now depends on property and living
wood of a different kind. No scheduled gathering, location, faction
law or magicks mechanism is established.

**Proposed local magicks: the dew witness.** A night-tender uses
available free-flowing aether to hold a little existing dew beneath
a branch. A trembling silver chain catches lamplight over a person
waiting to speak. It falls if the tender leaves or stops attending
to it. It can’t bend the wood, prolong the night, or keep a tree alive.

A seasonal worker asks to learn. If workers can keep their own
speaking order in view, a grower no longer has to be present to
acknowledge the next voice. One grower welcomes a chance to share
the duty. Another fears being held responsible for a gathering
she no longer oversees. The small spell gives someone responsibility
for a voice, not power over the orchard or an answer to the housing
dispute. It is not an approved spell or a source explanation.

## Marris Coast: the Surfwood

**Working name; not canon.** Beneath one wooded
[Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) headland, breakers hit the cliffs
without their crash. Walk inland and the treetops roar in the rhythm
of the waves. Voices and wind still sound where they originate.
The wood carries the surf’s sound; it does not make messages or
make the waves louder.

Only trees whose living roots reach the headland’s native rock take
part. Remove a connected stand and more of the ordinary noise
returns to the shore. Timber carried away has no such property.
The phenomenon’s cause and exact range are open.

**How it changed the harbour:** Repair crews could hear each
other’s warnings above the breakers. When the port once considered
taking the woodland for a larger timber trade, it chose the quiet
instead. Woodland households agreed to keep their connected stands
alive in exchange for a lasting say in whether any could be cut.
Their claim comes from the bargain, not ownership of the shore.

Today a repair master wants some of the wood made available so small
crews can afford sound boats. A woodland heir refuses: keeping
earshot of one another was part of how those crews stayed safe in the
first place. One living stand can’t both keep the harbour quiet and
become the boats it services. This is a proposed local struggle,
not a Marris-wide law, actual harbour, or playable facility.

### Surfwood: listen from both sides

**Two in-world explanations, neither established.** A woodland heir
says the living trees learned to take the waves inland. She points
to the silence of felled wood and doubts another tree could ever
replace a connected old stand. A repair master says the common thing
is the headland’s rock. Perhaps a different living stand could take its
place. He accepts louder surf in the meantime; she won’t promise
the quieter shore will return. Neither has proved that the trees or
the rock are the cause. Their uncertainty is part of the decision
about cutting, not a missing explanation the narrator must supply.

When one household passes on a stand, its next keeper listens under
the trees while a harbour household listens below the cliffs. They tell
each other afterwards what they could hear. The proposed custom ends
with a new promise to keep the trees. One daughter will care for them
this year but will not promise the same labour on behalf of children
she may have. She asks the harbour families to say what they will
do now. A young boat owner offers paid help, knowing it will delay
his own repairs. The quiet remains something both sides have to make
possible, not a gift one family is born owing.

## Northern kingdom: the Emberways

**Working name; not canon.** In one inland part of
[Iselin’s homeland](../../narrative/game_01/characters/ISELIN.md#northern-home-and-the-journey),
blue ice rises into branching vaults above gardens that stay green
through deep winter. An amber edge appears on the ice as warmth travels
from a distant hearth. The soil under the vault thaws; the ground
beside the hearth can stay bitterly cold.

The connected ice carries warmth that already exists. The source
cools faster, a broken branch no longer carries warmth to its end,
and a cut piece elsewhere is ordinary ice. No heat is created.
The origin and exact location remain open. The effect does not create
a northern approach or explain Iselin’s abilities or final arc.

**How it changed households:** In one proposed older winter, a ridge
collapse broke several branches. People moved their hearths to the
remaining connected ice and chose to live under one garden vault
instead of rebuilding separately. They dismantled their old houses
for fuel. Widowed neighbours and unrelated children became permanent
members of households that had once been separate.

Children still learn which hearth reaches which beds by walking
the branches with an elder. Joining a household can mean choosing
which hearth to tend and whose warmth you are responsible for. Now
a grower wants to reopen a dormant spur to her grandparents’ home so
she can raise her children without relatives’ permission. Doing so
would redirect part of the warmth that now reaches shared nursery
beds. Her relatives see that not as a private move but as a decision
about everyone’s winter. This is one proposed community, not a
kingdom-wide family rule.

## When two wonders teach different lessons

**Proposed encounter in Eldenmere, not an itinerary.** Two seasonal
workers consider signing a letter supporting the return of a
Nightglass house. One grew up at the Folding Orchards, where the
person currently sheltering beneath a crown speaks before it is
lifted. She wants the household living in the house now to answer
first. They are the ones who will wake beneath its roof tomorrow.

The other carries a dark wood mark with two keyholes. Her grandmother
gave up a key the winter the cliff turned clear. Letting present
occupants answer first, she says, makes it too easy to treat the
first family’s account as an interruption. They agree that no child
should be left homeless. They disagree about whether protecting
today’s home would repeat the old wrong. The letter is still
unsigned. One pulls up a chair; the other leaves it empty.

This tests what each person learned from a particular place, not
what everyone from either region believes. It makes no shared legal
rule, compulsory visit, new quest or travel connection.

## Merovar settlement history: three questions to resolve

The [foundational lore](../../FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md) establishes
independent successor cultures, not Merovar’s settlement sequence.
These are questions to develop, not consecutive eras.

| Question | Known anchor | Evidence the history would need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Where did communities endure?** | Elden Vale has headwater settlements; Lower Leth villages use higher ground above the floodplain. | Something that distinguishes long habitation from a later expansion. A good site alone cannot establish either. |
| **What made connections dependable?** | Greyfold passes depend on weather and upkeep; Rookfen channels change. | A known practice or arrangement that made repeat journeys possible. No route is required from geography alone. |
| **When did practices diverge?** | Wetter western Greyfold, dependably watered patches in Redstone, and Marris anchorages support different livelihoods. | A trace of people adapting at particular places, without making any way of life inevitable or continent-wide. |

## What present-day Merovar can tell us about contact

Look for what returns with people. The [Merovar atlas](MEROVAR.md)
establishes Marris households with relatives overseas and several
languages, and Greyfold apprentices coming back from river towns with
unfamiliar fashions. [Rimeward households](../POLAR_REGIONS.md#rimeward-shores)
keep relationships between seasonal herders and settled villages.
Repeated visits can explain why a practice looks familiar away from
home, without explaining who originally settled either place.

Keep the inland kingdom distinct. [Iselin’s homeland](../../narrative/game_01/characters/ISELIN.md#northern-home-and-the-journey)
is enclosed by mountains on three sides and approached from the south.
The Rimeward examples describe coastal and seasonal relationships.
Their proximity on an atlas doesn’t prove shared routes, government,
or identity. To reconstruct a past connection, look for corroborated
journeys, records that can be placed in sequence, or evidence of when
a practice appeared. Differences in words or habits alone aren’t enough.

## Elden Vale: mending before lighting

Some households clean or mend an older festival lantern in company. The work
gives a specific memory somewhere to begin without obliging anyone to speak.
A new lantern or no lantern is equally welcome. A neighbour may help at a
village worktable; an outlying family might prepare at home or receive a visitor
instead. Help begins by asking what should be left alone.

A young relative offers to replace a crooked panel. “Leave that one. Your aunt
never got it straight either.” The relative cleans the lamp instead. The flame
is ordinary; neither the light nor the repair guides the dead. The Sanctuary’s
distinct responsibility remains governed by the [faith guide](../ELDEN_VALE_FAITH.md)
and [covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md). This is not a change to
[Elira’s existing festival scenes](../../narrative/game_01/CH01_MQ04_PR2.md).

## Elden Vale: the basket that comes back

A neighbour asks for help with meals. A follower of
[Serene’s tradition](../ELDEN_VALE_FAITH.md) packs the vegetables.
Another neighbour, who doesn’t practise it, repairs the basket handle
and takes two vegetables out. “You’ll pull the bottom through.”
“She needs enough for tomorrow.” “Then we make two trips.”

The follower says the repairer has already done enough. “She waters my
beans when I’m away,” the repairer says. “And you walk too fast for
someone carrying soup.” When the basket comes back, there’s a spoon
tucked through the handle. The recipient noticed what they forgot.

The first neighbour sees a religious duty in the help. The other thinks
about the recipient and the beans. Neither needs to win an argument
about it. This is a proposed encounter with unnamed neighbours, not a
rite, a change to existing characters’ beliefs, or a substitute for the
Sanctuary’s duty to the dead.

## Elden Vale: Halden’s mill voice

A child has heard [Halden](../../narrative/game_01/CHAPTER_01.md) talking
over the working mill wheel often enough to think of it as one of his
voices. They meet him in a quiet place and tell him he’s speaking too
softly. “Do the mill voice.” He gives an absurdly thunderous greeting
and goes back to his conversation at an ordinary volume.

An adult hears machinery that makes everyone raise their voice. The
child hears a person they know becoming wonderfully different at work.
This is a proposed moment with an unnamed child, not a scripted task or
a change to Halden’s established work or the [mill](../regions/ELDEN_VALE.md).

## Elden Vale: what the closed passage remembers

The [public evidence in the covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md#the-public-evidence-behind-serenes-caution)
establishes injuries from an unexplained discharge when workers opened a
passage, followed by its closure. A plan is evidence of construction, not of
the discharge’s cause. This predates and does not explain the present crisis.

A keeper might preserve the boundary in the telling; a worker’s family might
preserve who came home injured. A road advocate could accept the account but
ask what it tells a present-day crew about safety. The differences concern
what people owe the injured and what a past accident can justify now. In Game I,
the public record and these different emphases are enough. Do not disclose a
cause, link the event to current incidents, substitute it for the Sanctuary’s
duty to the dead, or turn it into an Ancient clue. The

## Merovar’s Rimeward Shores: yesterday’s channel

A watcher sees an unfamiliar vessel approaching yesterday’s landing channel.
The boatbuilder, who usually sketches faces on scraps of plank while she
waits for seams to dry, takes one out with a drawing of the morning’s ice
instead. She shows the crew which landmarks they should no longer steer by. A grain consignee objects to
another delay. A deckhand whose brother worked the last unpaid rescue refuses
to lead the boat in before another approach is checked. The argument is over
who bears the cost of caution, not whether the strangers deserve warning.
This belongs to one shore settlement on Merovar’s side of the
[Rimeward Shores](../POLAR_REGIONS.md#rimeward-shores), not to a presumed
relation with Iselin’s kingdom.

## Merovar’s Rimeward Shores: the face that changes

In one household on [Merovar’s Rimeward Shores](../POLAR_REGIONS.md#rimeward-shores),
an older relative brings out a carved panel of the time the family’s
supper tipped over. A child gives the cook a chalk smile. Now the cook
is trying not to laugh instead of shouting. The relative tells the story
again as they remember it; the child gets to leave the smile. The chalk
will come off, and the carving will stay. Making a permanent cut in
someone else’s work takes a conversation.

A family with boatyard offcuts might keep several panels. Another
family might use drawings because they dislike altering old work.
The practice is a way for one household to make company on a visit,
not a test of craftsmanship or a custom shared by every northern shore.

## Marris Coast: the apprentice explains the repair

At one repair yard, an apprentice explains a first finished repair to the
supervisor and then to someone outside the yard, often from their household.
Pointing and demonstrating can bridge languages. The visitor can ask what
a customer would hesitate to ask, or spot something the supervisor missed.
A sibling covering a market shift may resent being asked to come too. Some
yards let a crewmate stand in; some use a paid trial instead. The custom does
not imply equal access to an apprenticeship or one uniform port culture.

## Marris Coast: the joke that comes home

At one [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) family’s welcome-home meal,
relatives act out a favourite harbour comedy. A pompous lover rehearses
a greeting without realizing he’s addressing a coat hung on the door.
Returning travellers bring different endings. Nobody agrees which
ending was there first.

An aunt loves hearing lines she remembers from childhood. Her nephew,
raised speaking another household language, enjoys the ridiculous bows
but misses the wordplay. When she explains one joke too many, he says,
“If you tell me when to laugh, I’ll charge admission.” She hands him
the coat. He makes it turn down the lover. This family can carry a joke
between languages without agreeing on which version counts as the original.

## Marris Coast: one place, several names

In one [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) port, a crew shortens a place-name
to shout it across a quay. A household keeps the version its relatives
use overseas. A ledger uses a fixed written entry. Before inventing a
variant, decide who says it to whom and whether the destination is
unambiguous. None of the three forms has to be oldest or most correct.

A returning apprentice gives the household form to a porter. He repeats
the working form so she knows what to say at the quay. A clerk can enter
both to make a delivery findable without requiring the family to change
how they speak at home. Do not infer an official language or a migration
history from the difference alone. Apply the previously reviewed
[place-naming transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mcKMbVXpRRA&t=687s)
on local and outsider usage, especially the later discussion of names
travelling with people.

## Marris Coast: the held answer

In one [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) sail-mending shed, a singer gives
two clipped notes and the others answer with one long note. A worker
draws thread through canvas and rests the needle against a thumbnail
while the answer sounds. It is familiar company, not a compulsory signal.

A returning relative carries the tune inland. Years later a member of
an [Elden Vale](../regions/ELDEN_VALE.md) household hums it while folding
laundry. They stretch the answering note until a child joins from
another room. At the coast the pause made room for another stitch;
here it lets a parent hear that a child is still listening. The two
uses can coexist without making the song an emblem of either region.

## Marris Coast: finish it how you like

At one [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) market, sellers serve cooked
fish with small dishes of salt and garden herbs on the counter. The
buyer does the last bit of seasoning. Children watch how their
household does it, then change it as soon as they get a plate of their own.

A warehouse worker asks for an herb by the name she uses at home. The
seller points to the wrong bowl. She takes a pinch, laughs, and says,
“That’s my mother’s. Mine has more of this.” Her brother wants his
plain. The custom works without requiring a shared name for every herb
or agreement about what tastes right. It belongs to one market, not
every Marris household.

## Marris Coast: the chest that opens the other way

A [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) joiner makes a pegged wooden chest
for bedding, not cargo. Its owner empties it to check the bottom and has
a loose hinge refastened rather than buying a new chest. When she moves
her bed, the lid can’t open from its old side. The repairer suggests
putting the bed back. She pays to move the hinges instead: “I sleep
better there.”

This is one furnishing in one proposed household. The repair records
the owner’s preference, not a coastal style, new asset or room layout.

## Marris Coast: who answers to First?

In one proposed repair yard, workers call whoever has the final check
that day “First.” The name gives an apprentice someone to ask before
a repair leaves the yard. A visitor hears “First, check this hinge”
and looks toward the supervisor. The youngest qualified person
answers instead. Another yard might just use personal names.

The form of address marks who has agreed to do a particular job, not
ownership, family rank, or seniority. The
[reviewed character-naming transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ikdyuJVq_p8&t=900s)
informed keeping the pattern in a small group and giving it a reason.

## Marris Coast: what counts as a fair repair

A [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) basket maker offers to replace a
fish seller’s carrying handle in exchange for patching some aprons.
The seller offers three aprons, counting holes. The maker asks for
two, pointing to the thick seam that will take longer. They inspect
both objects before agreeing.

When the fitted handle rubs the seller’s palm, the maker smooths it
without asking for another patch. They agreed on a handle someone
could carry, not just time spent repairing it. Neither owes the
other indefinite help. This is one exchange between neighbours,
not a local rate or a rule binding their trades.

## Marris Coast: say it back

At one [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) repair counter a customer
describes a broken shutter in the words she uses at home. A bilingual
neighbour helps. Before taking the job, the joiner asks which part must
still open, then repeats the request through the neighbour. The customer
corrects him: the lower panel must stay shut while the upper opens.
She wants daylight without rain on the bedding.

This is one shop’s habit of checking what someone means, not a wider
language claim. No one has to use a particular language to get the
repair. The [reviewed craft transcript](https://pickscribe.com/v/V2KpWOLTXx8)
informed starting with what the customer actually needs.

## Marris Coast and Elden Vale: Mira asks where the hand goes

In this voice test, a visiting apprentice describes a
[Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) yard where a first repair has to be
explained to someone outside the trade. [Mira](../../narrative/game_01/characters/MIRA.md)
stops turning a little wheel. “Even if your supervisor has checked it?”
The apprentice explains that his sister asked where a customer would
put their fingers. He moved the handle.

“Good,” Mira says. “Though I’d hate explaining something to Caelan
while he was trying to make it jam.” She shifts the wheel toward him.
“Here. Tell me where you’d put your hand. Before I tell you.”
Mira doesn’t adopt the whole custom or talk about it in the abstract;
she tries the part that could teach her something. No visit, task,
or change to her backstory is committed.

## Northern kingdom: who gets the next loaded cart?

At one winter dispatch review, local delegates bring names and blocked
journeys; Crown officers bring carts, material, and the crews available to
haul it. Keeping a through-road open may leave an outlying household without
medicine. Record both the agreed priority and the requests it puts off;
a visible promise can be challenged even when nobody can produce another
cart. This is a candidate local practice, not a constitution, a kingdom-wide
institution, or an excuse to bypass protected ground. It does not name the
kingdom, capital, roads, or officeholders. Do not use it to disclose the
Crown’s covert investigation or Iselin’s later fate.

## Northern kingdom: who checks your cuffs?

In one household in [Iselin’s northern kingdom](../../narrative/game_01/characters/ISELIN.md#northern-home-and-the-journey),
mittens hang over a bench with the cuffs turned back. Before a child
leaves, someone checks that the mittens cover the ends of the sleeves.
A child insists on checking an older brother, who stoops solemnly despite
being perfectly capable himself. Their grandmother stitched a crooked
square inside one mitten so the child can tell their own pair from
everybody else’s. Now the child wants the square on the outside where
people can see it.

Next door, everyone dresses themselves. Help is offered when requested,
not assumed. The same cold gives both families a reason to check their
clothes but doesn’t tell them how to show care. This is a domestic
proposal for one inland community, not a custom of the whole kingdom
or a claim about the Rimeward Shores. The [actual craft transcript](https://pickscribe.com/v/V2KpWOLTXx8)
informed the use of a concrete object to show the relationship.

## Northern kingdom: the board that isn’t ready

In one proposed inland workshop in [Iselin’s homeland](../../narrative/game_01/characters/ISELIN.md#northern-home-and-the-journey),
joiners set an offcut beside each stack of drying boards. Before
starting a door an apprentice splits the sample. The inside of the
cheaper stack’s offcut still feels damp. The joiner shows the customer
rather than making a promise about the whole stack. The apprentice
keeps the sample to compare later.

What matters here is a small difference in patience: to the customer
the stack looks ready now, while the apprentice has decided what
they want to check again. The [reviewed craft transcript](https://pickscribe.com/v/V2KpWOLTXx8)
informed the use of a concrete detail. This is neither a northern
standard nor a new workshop placement.

## Northern contact: what a supplier description could show

household on [Merovar’s Rimeward Shores](../POLAR_REGIONS.md#rimeward-shores).

## Elden Vale: naming places without inventing their history

Use three registers: short inherited settlement names, plain working names
such as Upper Millrace, and public institutions with clear descriptors such
as Elden Vale Sanctuary. Before suggesting a name, write one sentence:
**who uses it, what they need to distinguish, and what still has meaning
to them**. A road name can be straightforward; an inherited name can
plausibly wear down over time. Do not add a founder, migration, battle or
language family merely to explain a sound.

Favor recognizable sounds and distinct opening syllables for places discussed
together. Leave existing names alone. Do not overuse *Elden-*, *-mere*, or
sacred terms. Eldenmere does not imply a lake or shore; neither *Elden* nor
*Leth* has an approved etymology. The southern town and boundary waters stay
unnamed in this pass. This is a Vale guide, not a Merovar-wide naming grammar.

## Merovar: naming people for dialogue

Keep the readable range already present in the [cast](../../narrative/game_01/CHARACTERS.md):
Caelan, Mira, Iselin, Liam and the Venn family sit alongside Lucan Rooke,
Rhea Sorel and Edran Hale. A recurring sound or surname structure can signal

## Peoples are not species, and regions are not personalities

The [Merovar atlas](MEROVAR.md) distinguishes communities,
livelihoods and ties without assigning ancestry, morality or magical
potential by region. Human NPCs are established in the
[region roster](../../REGION_NPCS.md). The sampled canon establishes neither a
nonhuman sapient roster nor a human-only world.

Before adding a nonhuman people, try a non-canon alternate version of an
existing conflict. In the Lower Leth weighing dispute, two residents with
the same visibly nonhuman appearance take different sides, and humans also
divide on the issue. Give each person a preference unrelated to the dispute.
Can a reader explain all four positions from relationships and material
stakes? Does the visual choice add anything once that test passes? If not,
keep the scene with the current cast rather than create a species to fill
a category. No name, biology, homeland, magical affinity or Ancient origin
is assigned here.

Craft source: [Hello Future Me, *Fantasy and Alien Races*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aOa4O_M9ic)
(644k indexed views; [timestamped transcript mirror](https://lilys.ai/ko/notes/772862)
reviewed). At 09:54–15:23 the useful lessons are variation within a group
and the limits of defining people by habitat or biology; neither implies
a particular species belongs in this game.

### Optional test: resemblance is not familiarity

Try two alternate versions of the same small encounters. In the
Rookfen bedtime-screen scene, a visiting adult closes the screen while
the child is still talking. The child opens it again and the visitor
stays to listen. At the Marris welcome-home meal, the same visitor knows
the coat joke already while someone else needs it demonstrated.

In one version, the visitor and the child have a shared nonhuman
appearance and the unfamiliar guest at the meal is human. In the other,
everyone is human. Does resemblance make the difference between looking
like family and knowing how a household works more apparent? If it
doesn’t, keep the all-human version. Neither version defines a species,
a migration, a homeland or an ancestry for any established character.
This applies the previously reviewed
[transcript on fantasy peoples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aOa4O_M9ic&t=594s)
to the narrower question of how familiarity is learned.

### Optional appearance candidate: two ways to wear a hood

In one alternate version of the Lower Leth weighing dispute, two residents
have short, swept-back horns. A pullover hood catches. One resident wears
a hood that unbuttons, still stitched unevenly by a relative. The other
uses a wrap because it’s faster to put on. No garment is an ancestral
sign or a practice shared by everyone with that appearance.

They take opposite sides on when to weigh the grain, as do two human
participants. Neither preference comes from anatomy. Try the same scene
with an all-human cast and keep this candidate only if the visible
adaptation adds useful information about the two individuals.

## Before a proposal becomes canon

1. **Find the use.** Say what would change for a particular person or
   place and why the idea is not already served by an approved scene.
2. **Name the choice.** Write down the smallest unresolved decision:
   a relationship, an event, a name, a route assumption, or something
   else the proposal actually needs. Leave unrelated questions open.
3. **Protect boundaries.** Check who knows what, what remains a belief,
   and whether a route, name or scene would require an unapproved
   map or story change.
4. **Move, don’t duplicate.** After approval, put the accepted fact
   in its existing authoritative home. Remove any duplicate here
   and link to the owner. Merging a proposal is not that approval.

2. Test how someone could understand one wonder’s rule without an

## Map implications for a later review

- A writer-only **Upper Leth haulage overlay** could use two kinds of marks.

  the walls from the southern land checkpoint associated with relief traffic.
  Both checkpoints remain closed during the opening quests; the writer’s
  overlay cannot make them traversable. Do not draw an eastern bridge. **Constraint notes** distinguish the brook,
  controlled millrace, unnamed boundary rivers and unnamed downstream
  tributaries. The nearer seam is protected, not an authorized supply;
  farther stone remains available and takes longer to haul. Omit a mark
  if it only works by assuming an unestablished crossing, navigable reach,
  quarry, or excavation permission. Apply the actual-crossing check from
  the [reviewed map transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pjLSv4WNUtY)
  to the [overworld](../../OVERWORLD.md) and the
  [covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md), which remain authoritative.
- A writer-only **Rookfen–Marris cargo overlay** has three review gates,
  distinct from the Upper Leth road proposal:
  1. **Delta passage.** Check that a local transfer point has a usable
     passage for the likely carrier under the intended conditions. A
     marker by itself is not evidence that the channel is navigable.
  2. **Coastal approach.** Check how the coastal leg would reach a
     receiving Marris port, including tides and the rough scale of the
     voyage. A delta landing and a seaport needn’t be adjacent; the port
     is not assumed to sit at the Leth’s mouth.
  3. **Working agreement.** Identify who might accept cargo, deal with
     delays, and grant access at each end. A plausible route doesn’t
     establish a service, a tariff, or a common government.

### Constraints a later writer’s overlay must preserve

- **Nightglass:** the view appears in the sunless season. Transparency
  is neither an entrance nor evidence that a terrace will collapse.
- **Reedwake:** only particular ground moves under the proposal’s
  tidal conditions. A dock alone cannot label where its people now live.
- **Emberways:** warmth follows a connected formation and cools the
  source. A break or detached piece cannot feed the same beds.

Keep season, reach and the observed rule attached to each proposal.
