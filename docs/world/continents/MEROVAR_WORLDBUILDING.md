## Elden Vale: mending before lighting

Some households clean or mend an older festival lantern in company. The work
gives a specific memory somewhere to begin without obliging anyone to speak.
A new lantern or no lantern is equally welcome. A neighbour may help at a
village worktable; an outlying family might prepare at home or receive a visitor
instead. Help begins by asking what should be left alone.

A young relative offers to replace a crooked panel. “Leave that one. Your aunt
never got it straight either.” The relative cleans the lamp instead. The flame
is ordinary; neither the light nor the repair guides the dead. The Sanctuary’s
distinct responsibility remains governed by the [faith guide](../ELDEN_VALE_FAITH.md)
and [covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md). This is not a change to
[Elira’s existing festival scenes](../../narrative/game_01/CH01_MQ04_PR2.md).

## Elden Vale: the basket that comes back

A neighbour asks for help with meals. A follower of
[Serene’s tradition](../ELDEN_VALE_FAITH.md) packs the vegetables.
Another neighbour, who doesn’t practise it, repairs the basket handle
and takes two vegetables out. “You’ll pull the bottom through.”
“She needs enough for tomorrow.” “Then we make two trips.”

The follower says the repairer has already done enough. “She waters my
beans when I’m away,” the repairer says. “And you walk too fast for
someone carrying soup.” When the basket comes back, there’s a spoon
tucked through the handle. The recipient noticed what they forgot.

The first neighbour sees a religious duty in the help. The other thinks
about the recipient and the beans. Neither needs to win an argument
about it. This is a proposed encounter with unnamed neighbours, not a
rite, a change to existing characters’ beliefs, or a substitute for the
Sanctuary’s duty to the dead.

## Elden Vale: what the closed passage remembers

The [public evidence in the covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md#the-public-evidence-behind-serenes-caution)
establishes injuries from an unexplained discharge when workers opened a
passage, followed by its closure. A plan is evidence of construction, not of
the discharge’s cause. This predates and does not explain the present crisis.

A keeper might preserve the boundary in the telling; a worker’s family might
preserve who came home injured. A road advocate could accept the account but
ask what it tells a present-day crew about safety. The differences concern
what people owe the injured and what a past accident can justify now. In Game I,
the public record and these different emphases are enough. Do not disclose a
cause, link the event to current incidents, substitute it for the Sanctuary’s
duty to the dead, or turn it into an Ancient clue. The
[Revelation Plan](../../narrative/saga/REVELATION_PLAN.md) governs disclosure.

## Elden Vale: Iselin under pressure

[Iselin’s character sheet](../../narrative/game_01/characters/ISELIN.md) and
[MQ03](../../narrative/game_01/CH01_MQ03.md) show a regent who counts the real
cost of delay, then acts as though counting those costs entitles her to decide
who bears them. Distant stone costs carts, money and time. The protected seam
is nearer, not free. The order comes from the queen; Iselin gives the crew orders and chooses to
negotiate without disclosing the deployment. Show both responsibilities. Her subsequent
accountability matters without undoing the injury or breach of trust.

A voice test, not new script: “I kept counting the people waiting for the stone.
I should have counted the people I was asking to bear its cost.” Preserve the
opening’s established limits on what the party knows about her.

## Elden Vale: the notice already posted

In a proposed relief visit, [Iselin’s](../../narrative/game_01/characters/ISELIN.md)
page puts up a notice for interviews in the Sanctuary hall before checking
with the keepers. A keeper takes it down: families are expected there for
remembrance that afternoon. Several petitioners have already travelled.
A bereaved carrier offers to move her own visit, not everyone else’s.
Another petitioner will miss paid work if the interviews start later.

Iselin asks what hours the hall can actually offer. She has the page
correct the notice and tells the people already waiting herself. The
result shows one boundary without declaring a new law: she can direct
her relief work, but a notice from her office does not supply the
keeper’s agreement. This is not an MQ03 beat, a new required visit,
or a permanent veto. The [reviewed transcript](https://pickscribe.com/v/V2KpWOLTXx8)
informed showing politics through a problem the people must actually solve.

## Upper Leth approach: the unnamed town south of Eldenmere

One proposed identity for the next town south is a market that suits
small consignments. A household can sell a few baskets, arrange for a
carrier to take its wool with someone else’s load, and buy a tool or
have a cart repaired without making a longer trip. The place people
point to is a loading yard with a covered waiting edge. There are
baskets under the benches, harness drying above them, and someone
mindful of the next household's goods while they do their shopping.

The [Vale guide](../regions/ELDEN_VALE.md#neighbours-and-travel) puts the
destination on the way toward the upper Leth market towns. The
[overworld guide](../../OVERWORLD.md) keeps the far-bank road of the
damaged western crossing distinct from the southern checkpoint.
The market does not establish a new bridge, a ford, a navigable
waterway, a playable layout, or the identity of a larger Upper Leth town.

**Name tests, not assignments:** **Tarrow** is an inherited-sounding
settlement name without a claimed derivation; **Loadstead** is a more
literal working name; **the Yard** is what locals might call the loading
place, not necessarily the town. “We’ll take it to Tarrow” sounds distinct
from a line naming Eldenmere. I favour *Tarrow* as a test and *the Yard*
as a local nickname if the town’s market role is accepted. The
[reviewed naming transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mcKMbVXpRRA&t=455s)
informed testing how a name wears in use. Canon remains unnamed.

## Upper Leth: the stone-use bench

One upper Leth market town brings a receiving mason, a carrier and a
clerk together when there is a dispute over what a delivery can be used
for. A block a mason refuses for a crossing might be useful as broken
stone in a drain. The bench decides what can be salvaged without
pretending the crossing has received what it needs. Its judgments are
local to the work, not Merovar-wide grades.

When the mason rejects a load, the carrier wants the cart empty in time
for another paid run. A drain worker offers to take the fragments. A
quarry worker objects to being billed for bad stone when it might have
cracked on the road. The clerk keeps one marked fragment and records who
refused it, for what use, and which claims are still disputed. That small
piece lets a later visitor see why one repair went ahead while the other
still waited.

**Canon boundary:** [Elden Vale’s regional setting](../regions/ELDEN_VALE.md#authority-development-and-disagreement)
establishes the road and quarry districts; the
[covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md) leaves distant stone available but costly.
Reusing a disputed delivery neither replaces a quarry nor gives anyone
permission to work protected ground. The bench is not part of MQ03.
The [worldbuilding lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V2KpWOLTXx8)
noted under the northern kingdom informed the scene’s emphasis on what
the institution can and cannot do for these particular workers.

## Greyfold Uplands: Rhea revises her own theory

[Rhea’s established background](../../narrative/game_01/COMPANIONS.md)
gives her local magick instruction and years studying streams, storms and
forests. For a voice test, let her eagerly explain an odd current, then
strike “therefore” from her notebook and ask Caelan what he saw. Her
curiosity gets ahead of her conclusions; asking for an independent
observation is the habit that pulls her back. That tension belongs to Rhea,
not to everyone raised on the western slopes.

## Greyfold Uplands: who gets the remaining fuel?

A missed winter delivery leaves a shared roadside shelter short. A shepherd
wants fuel left for neighbours returning from exposed grazing. A carrier
needs it now for passengers stranded by weather. The shelter keeper measures
out an immediate share and records what the carrier can bring on a return
journey. The note is an obligation to the next guest, not a condition of being
allowed to warm up. A paid inn may offer a different agreement, and sanctuary
hospitality frames help as service. These are alternatives, not grades of
generosity; do not extend the Elden Vale covenant across Greyfold.

## Greyfold Uplands: the verse you change

In one [Greyfold](MEROVAR.md#greyfold-uplands) hall, a familiar
unaccompanied song ends with a verse the singer makes up. Listeners try to
catch three household details tucked into a journey: a lid that never
fits, an uncle’s snoring, the cheese nobody admits finishing. Children
count discoveries on their fingers. Whoever spots the last detail chooses
the next singer, who is allowed to pass.

Some older singers favour details everybody knows. Apprentices returning
from river towns bring jokes their relatives don’t get. One sings about
the battered pot in their rented room. Nobody laughs. Their grandmother
asks what they cooked in it, and someone calls out a rhyme for the answer.
The verse sticks because the room learns something new. Another hall
might prefer to end the song without a game.

## Greyfold Uplands: the unfinished house

In one [Greyfold](MEROVAR.md#greyfold-uplands) settlement, a makers’ circle
furnishes a miniature house with scraps. A shepherd makes chairs; a retired
quarry worker sews curtains. Anyone who comes can contribute and nobody
has to explain an absence. A person's usual trade doesn't decide what
they make for this house.

Returning apprentices want to add a balcony they saw in a river town.
A founding member says the house no longer looks like home. Someone
remembers that the original roof copied an inn none of them had
visited. They keep the roof beside the model and add the balcony.
The founder stops coming. Their little chair stays. This is one
voluntary circle, not a Greyfold-wide identity or a guild.

## Lower Leth: a fair weight after a flood

At one weighing house, the keeper deducts the sack’s weight in front of
witnesses trusted by the grower and buyer. A disputed sack stays tied. Each
party holds one half of a split tally, cut with matching marks. The farmer
needs grain sold today to buy food. The buyer wants to weigh it after drying
rather than pay for water; the boat crew cannot wait another day without
losing a run. Witnesses can settle what the scale read, not who can afford the
delay. This is one town’s practice, not the river’s law.

## Lower Leth: who speaks after the water

The [Lower Leth atlas entry](MEROVAR.md#lower-leth) establishes flood risks,
mixed landholding and market-day plays. In this proposed local history,
residents opened an embankment during a flood to spare a market settlement.
Several tenant households lost homes and orchards. At later flood-planning
meetings, displaced households customarily spoke first, even if they no
longer held land. This is a local practice, not a legal right elsewhere.

One market family remembers a necessary breach and the proof that ordinary
people could change public decisions. A displaced family remembers being
pressed to agree and later thanked in public, with nowhere to return.
Some descendants insist on their speaking turn. Others resent being asked
to tell the same story every year. Both accounts can be true without anyone
denying that the breach saved lives.

A travelling teacher rehearses a play about the flood. A former pupil
objects: the displaced family goes offstage smiling and never comes back.
After his established introduction, [Edran](../../narrative/game_01/COMPANIONS.md#edran-hale)
could hear the argument and ask whose account supplied the ending. The
teacher decides what to change. The party need not travel to the Lower
Leth, the incident does not explain any current anomaly, and neither the
performance nor the meeting is a committed quest.

## Lower Leth: the crossed-out column

**Proposed history:** In one [Lower Leth](MEROVAR.md#lower-leth) town,
families persuaded a school to stop requiring a settled household to
vouch for each pupil. Children of tenants or travelling workers could
enrol directly. The old admission ledger still has a ruled-out
“household sponsor” column and later entries without sponsors. This
changed one school’s practice, not regional law.

An older resident remembers carrying their slate through the door
instead of waiting for someone to speak for them. Their grandchild
remembers a cousin who got a place but missed lessons for work. “They
stopped asking whose child you were. They never asked who could spare
you.” Game I could encounter the ledger or either account without a
flashback or a new visit. Edran need not have attended the school or
taken part in the change. Neither account explains a current anomaly.

The [actual Sanderson transcript](https://pickscribe.com/v/V2KpWOLTXx8)
informed using a concrete object and individual viewpoints. It supplies
no facts about the school. Keep testimony and established history
separate under the [revelation plan](../../narrative/saga/REVELATION_PLAN.md).

## Lower Leth: Edran’s borrowed lesson book

A clinic colleague of [Edran](../../narrative/game_01/COMPANIONS.md#edran-hale)
thinks an apprentice needs enough time with one teacher to be watched
making the same mistake twice. Edran wants training to reach people who
cannot leave their settlements. He trusts her judgment in part because
former pupils come back to teach beside her.

They pass a battered lesson book back and forth. She crosses out examples
that assume a room or equipment a village might not have. He adds questions
he hears in smaller settlements. Each keeps the other’s corrections,
including the irritating ones. She makes room for visiting learners. When
Edran is in town, he covers one of her teaching afternoons so she can leave
early. The exchange is a proposed relationship, not a fixed scene or an
earlier introduction, and neither person considers the argument settled.

## Rookfen and Marris: where a cargo changes hands

At an unnamed Rookfen landing, grain coming down the Leth is checked for damp
and entered on matching tallies before a shallow-draft carrier takes it
through a locally known channel. A later coastal leg can reach an unnamed
Marris seaport; salt may travel in the opposite direction. This is one possible
service, not a fixed route on the map. It does not place the Marris port at the
mouth of the Leth or put the two communities under one government.

When a shifted channel marker interrupts the handoff, the landing needs dry
storage, the boat crew loses earning time, and the port loses a cargo its
workers expected. Replacing the marker restores passage, but does not decide
who pays. The [World Atlas](../WORLD_ATLAS.md) is the source for commodities
and geography; the transfer, voyage and dispute are proposals.

## Rookfen: the bedtime screen

In one [Rookfen](MEROVAR.md#rookfen) household, a child turns a patterned
reed screen across the sleeping corner in the evening and hooks its
cord loops over smooth pegs. The lowest loop is wrapped in soft cloth
where small fingers pull. A hand’s-width gap means “stay and talk.”
Closing it means “goodnight,” even when the adults stay awake nearby.
The child can ask for privacy without having to send anyone away.

Neighbours who only put up a screen when guests stay over would not
read the gap the same way. A more crowded household might have no room
to divide at all. The signal belongs to this family, not the pattern
or the delta.

## Rookfen: letters without an introduction

**Proposed history:** Between two [Rookfen](MEROVAR.md#rookfen) settlements
already connected by visiting evenings, someone once had to find a
relative willing to carry a letter. Hosts started taking small bundles
for the next visiting boat instead. A patched cloth wrapper still shows
the crossed-out names of the households that have looked after it.
The change used an existing connection, not a new channel or a formal post.

An older reed worker remembers no longer saving her news until a cousin
travelled. A neighbour misses being able to give a delicate message
some context before it was read. Now people expect a letter to travel
without a personal favour, but a volunteer still has to be there to
collect it. The [reviewed craft transcript](https://pickscribe.com/v/V2KpWOLTXx8)
informed letting one ordinary object show what changed.

## Redstone March: the first drink

One farming town offers a thirsty visitor a first drink without charge.
Watering sale animals or filling a caravan’s barrels draws on a larger supply
and is counted and paid for. When a carrier’s only working mule collapses,
a resident can vouch for emergency water; the carrier repays the town by
cleaning the cistern. A merchant objects to the keeper’s discretion, while a
poor carrier knows the difference between an exception and losing a
livelihood. Another town might handle the same emergency differently.
“March” still does not establish a frontier or a shared water law.

## Redstone March: the losing move

At one shaded [Redstone](MEROVAR.md#redstone-march) market, both players
in a board game are invited to name the other’s best move before the
winner is called. Spectators argue over whether the praise is earned.
A fruit seller sets aside the most bruised pear as a prize for a fine
speech about a terrible game. One regular likes finding something kind
to say; his sister thinks a loser should be able to leave without a
public lesson. Travellers sometimes skip the speeches altogether.

A young repairer beats the merchant whose recommendation she needs
for her first job on her own. He praises a move she knows was a mistake.
Rather than correcting him in front of the market, she sets the pieces
back and asks him to show her what he saw. Now he can change his mind
without having to apologize, and the market has something new to argue
about before work resumes.

## Redstone March: Lucan gives the account before the title

[Lucan](../../narrative/game_01/characters/LUCAN.md) takes pride in
bringing people through safely. For a character test, have a returning
escort in one [Redstone](MEROVAR.md#redstone-march) community describe the
journey before anyone praises it. A carrier interrupts Lucan’s polished
account to ask where the road narrowed. He hears doubt about his
competence. She is taking a wider wagon tomorrow and needs to know
whether she can use the same road.

“I brought everyone through,” he says. “And tomorrow I take a wider
wagon,” she answers. He marks the narrowest stretch from memory and
asks the younger escort to check it. When corrected, he moves the mark
without making a speech. He still straightens when the carrier thanks
him. This is a voice test, not a fixed hometown custom or mission.

## Redstone March: the second household

Two households in different [Redstone](MEROVAR.md#redstone-march)
settlements have taken turns hosting each other’s young people for two
generations. A caravan couple’s daughter stays with a farming family
while learning wagon repair; the hosts’ son later travels with the
caravan family. Nobody takes over parental authority or promises a
marriage. Years later the daughter still sends the farming grandmother
descriptions of weddings she attends.

The son returns with a partner who dislikes being introduced as a
“borrowed child.” His mother means affection. His partner hears a
reminder they have never been family. The grandmother pulls up a
chair and asks how they would like to be introduced. The connection
grows through invitations and time spent together, not shared descent
or an obligation other households must follow.

## Merovar’s Rimeward Shores: yesterday’s channel

A watcher sees an unfamiliar vessel approaching yesterday’s landing channel.
The boatbuilder, who usually sketches faces on scraps of plank while she
waits for seams to dry, takes one out with a drawing of the morning’s ice
instead. She shows the crew which landmarks they should no longer steer by. A grain consignee objects to
another delay. A deckhand whose brother worked the last unpaid rescue refuses
to lead the boat in before another approach is checked. The argument is over
who bears the cost of caution, not whether the strangers deserve warning.
This belongs to one shore settlement on Merovar’s side of the
[Rimeward Shores](../POLAR_REGIONS.md#rimeward-shores), not to a presumed
relation with Iselin’s kingdom.

## Merovar’s Rimeward Shores: the face that changes

In one household on [Merovar’s Rimeward Shores](../POLAR_REGIONS.md#rimeward-shores),
an older relative brings out a carved panel of the time the family’s
supper tipped over. A child gives the cook a chalk smile. Now the cook
is trying not to laugh instead of shouting. The relative tells the story
again as they remember it; the child gets to leave the smile. The chalk
will come off, and the carving will stay. Making a permanent cut in
someone else’s work takes a conversation.

A family with boatyard offcuts might keep several panels. Another
family might use drawings because they dislike altering old work.
The practice is a way for one household to make company on a visit,
not a test of craftsmanship or a custom shared by every northern shore.

## Marris Coast: the apprentice explains the repair

At one repair yard, an apprentice explains a first finished repair to the
supervisor and then to someone outside the yard, often from their household.
Pointing and demonstrating can bridge languages. The visitor can ask what
a customer would hesitate to ask, or spot something the supervisor missed.
A sibling covering a market shift may resent being asked to come too. Some
yards let a crewmate stand in; some use a paid trial instead. The custom does
not imply equal access to an apprenticeship or one uniform port culture.

## Marris Coast: the joke that comes home

At one [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) family’s welcome-home meal,
relatives act out a favourite harbour comedy. A pompous lover rehearses
a greeting without realizing he’s addressing a coat hung on the door.
Returning travellers bring different endings. Nobody agrees which
ending was there first.

An aunt loves hearing lines she remembers from childhood. Her nephew,
raised speaking another household language, enjoys the ridiculous bows
but misses the wordplay. When she explains one joke too many, he says,
“If you tell me when to laugh, I’ll charge admission.” She hands him
the coat. He makes it turn down the lover. This family can carry a joke
between languages without agreeing on which version counts as the original.

## Marris Coast: one place, several names

In one [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) port, a crew shortens a place-name
to shout it across a quay. A household keeps the version its relatives
use overseas. A ledger uses a fixed written entry. Before inventing a
variant, decide who says it to whom and whether the destination is
unambiguous. None of the three forms has to be oldest or most correct.

A returning apprentice gives the household form to a porter. He repeats
the working form so she knows what to say at the quay. A clerk can enter
both to make a delivery findable without requiring the family to change
how they speak at home. Do not infer an official language or a migration
history from the difference alone. Apply the previously reviewed
[place-naming transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mcKMbVXpRRA&t=687s)
on local and outsider usage, especially the later discussion of names
travelling with people.

## Marris Coast: the held answer

In one [Marris](MEROVAR.md#marris-coast) sail-mending shed, a singer gives
two clipped notes and the others answer with one long note. A worker
draws thread through canvas and rests the needle against a thumbnail
while the answer sounds. It is familiar company, not a compulsory signal.

A returning relative carries the tune inland. Years later a member of
an [Elden Vale](../regions/ELDEN_VALE.md) household hums it while folding
laundry. They stretch the answering note until a child joins from
another room. At the coast the pause made room for another stitch;
here it lets a parent hear that a child is still listening. The two
uses can coexist without making the song an emblem of either region.

## Northern kingdom: who gets the next loaded cart?

At one winter dispatch review, local delegates bring names and blocked
journeys; Crown officers bring carts, material, and the crews available to
haul it. Keeping a through-road open may leave an outlying household without
medicine. Record both the agreed priority and the requests it puts off;
a visible promise can be challenged even when nobody can produce another
cart. This is a candidate local practice, not a constitution, a kingdom-wide
institution, or an excuse to bypass protected ground. It does not name the
kingdom, capital, roads, or officeholders. Do not use it to disclose the
Crown’s covert investigation or Iselin’s later fate.

Craft source for the thin northern institutional material: [Brandon Sanderson,
*Worldbuilding Part Two*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V2KpWOLTXx8)
(838k indexed views; [transcript mirror](https://pickscribe.com/v/V2KpWOLTXx8)
reviewed). The useful technique is to tie one physical constraint to a
specific cultural or institutional consequence a character can experience.
It is not evidence for any northern relief institution.

## Northern kingdom: who checks your cuffs?

In one household in [Iselin’s northern kingdom](../../narrative/game_01/characters/ISELIN.md#northern-home-and-the-journey),
mittens hang over a bench with the cuffs turned back. Before a child
leaves, someone checks that the mittens cover the ends of the sleeves.
A child insists on checking an older brother, who stoops solemnly despite
being perfectly capable himself. Their grandmother stitched a crooked
square inside one mitten so the child can tell their own pair from
everybody else’s. Now the child wants the square on the outside where
people can see it.

Next door, everyone dresses themselves. Help is offered when requested,
not assumed. The same cold gives both families a reason to check their
clothes but doesn’t tell them how to show care. This is a domestic
proposal for one inland community, not a custom of the whole kingdom
or a claim about the Rimeward Shores. The [actual craft transcript](https://pickscribe.com/v/V2KpWOLTXx8)
informed the use of a concrete object to show the relationship.

## Elden Vale: naming places without inventing their history

Use three registers: short inherited settlement names, plain working names
such as Upper Millrace, and public institutions with clear descriptors such
as Elden Vale Sanctuary. Before suggesting a name, write one sentence:
**who uses it, what they need to distinguish, and what still has meaning
to them**. A road name can be straightforward; an inherited name can
plausibly wear down over time. Do not add a founder, migration, battle or
language family merely to explain a sound.

Favor recognizable sounds and distinct opening syllables for places discussed
together. Leave existing names alone. Do not overuse *Elden-*, *-mere*, or
sacred terms. Eldenmere does not imply a lake or shore; neither *Elden* nor
*Leth* has an approved etymology. The southern town and boundary waters stay
unnamed in this pass. This is a Vale guide, not a Merovar-wide naming grammar.

## Merovar: naming people for dialogue

Keep the readable range already present in the [cast](../../narrative/game_01/CHARACTERS.md):
Caelan, Mira, Iselin, Liam and the Venn family sit alongside Lucan Rooke,
Rhea Sorel and Edran Hale. A recurring sound or surname structure can signal
a particular family or institution when the story gives it a reason. The
three established companion surnames do not establish three regional
languages or a continent-wide rule about inheritance.

Before choosing a name, read it aloud in two short exchanges with the people
who share its scene. Check opening sound, rhythm and written shape. Similar
initials are fine if the names are still easy to distinguish. Keep current
spellings and leave unassigned surnames open until a character needs one.

Craft source: [Jed Herne, *The Easy Way to Name Your Fantasy Characters*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ikdyuJVq_p8)
(508k views when checked; video transcript reviewed): [15:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ikdyuJVq_p8&t=900s)
on smaller naming groups, [19:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ikdyuJVq_p8&t=1169s)
on reasons for patterns, and [22:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ikdyuJVq_p8&t=1344s)
on distinguishing a cast. Treat unique initials as a useful check, not a law.

## Peoples are not species, and regions are not personalities

The [Merovar atlas](MEROVAR.md) distinguishes communities,
livelihoods and ties without assigning ancestry, morality or magical
potential by region. Human NPCs are established in the
[region roster](../../REGION_NPCS.md). The sampled canon establishes neither a
nonhuman sapient roster nor a human-only world.

Before adding a nonhuman people, try a non-canon alternate version of an
existing conflict. In the Lower Leth weighing dispute, two residents with
the same visibly nonhuman appearance take different sides, and humans also
divide on the issue. Give each person a preference unrelated to the dispute.
Can a reader explain all four positions from relationships and material
stakes? Does the visual choice add anything once that test passes? If not,
keep the scene with the current cast rather than create a species to fill
a category. No name, biology, homeland, magical affinity or Ancient origin
is assigned here.

Craft source: [Hello Future Me, *Fantasy and Alien Races*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aOa4O_M9ic)
(644k indexed views; [timestamped transcript mirror](https://lilys.ai/ko/notes/772862)
reviewed). At 09:54–15:23 the useful lessons are variation within a group
and the limits of defining people by habitat or biology; neither implies
a particular species belongs in this game.

### Optional test: resemblance is not familiarity

Try two alternate versions of the same small encounters. In the
Rookfen bedtime-screen scene, a visiting adult closes the screen while
the child is still talking. The child opens it again and the visitor
stays to listen. At the Marris welcome-home meal, the same visitor knows
the coat joke already while someone else needs it demonstrated.

In one version, the visitor and the child have a shared nonhuman
appearance and the unfamiliar guest at the meal is human. In the other,
everyone is human. Does resemblance make the difference between looking
like family and knowing how a household works more apparent? If it
doesn’t, keep the all-human version. Neither version defines a species,
a migration, a homeland or an ancestry for any established character.
This applies the previously reviewed
[transcript on fantasy peoples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aOa4O_M9ic&t=594s)
to the narrower question of how familiarity is learned.

## Map implications for a later review

- A writer-only **Upper Leth haulage overlay** could use two kinds of marks.
  **Passage-state marks** distinguish the ruined western bridge road inside
  the walls from the southern land checkpoint that relief traffic uses.
  Do not draw an eastern bridge. **Constraint notes** distinguish the brook,
  controlled millrace, unnamed boundary rivers and unnamed downstream
  tributaries. The nearer seam is protected, not an authorized supply;
  farther stone remains available and takes longer to haul. Omit a mark
  if it only works by assuming an unestablished crossing, navigable reach,
  quarry, or excavation permission. Apply the actual-crossing check from
  the [reviewed map transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pjLSv4WNUtY)
  to the [overworld](../../OVERWORLD.md) and the
  [covenant](../SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md), which remain authoritative.
- A writer-only **Rookfen–Marris cargo overlay** could distinguish a delta
  transfer, a coastal leg, and a receiving seaport. Keep its route provisional
  until channel, tide and distance assumptions are checked. It does not move
  a coast, redraw the classroom map, or require an overseas leg.

These are questions for a later map decision, not instructions to alter the
game’s map. Regions outside Merovar are unchanged.
