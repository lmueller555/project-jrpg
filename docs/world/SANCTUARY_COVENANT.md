# Sanctuary Covenant — Elden Vale

**Status:** Approved setting and narrative canon, 2026-10-31.

**Implementation:** MQ03's runtime dialogue now presents the approved relief
needs, public records, protected boundary and unresolved trial-cut request.
The later authorization and excavation remain separate future story work.

**Scope:** The protected ridge sanctuary near Eldenmere, its public covenant,
and the legitimate disagreement between Serene and Aldric in Chapter 1.

This guide records the approved local facts for reuse in later scenes. It
expands the [Elden Vale regional setting](regions/ELDEN_VALE.md) and informs
[MQ03 — The Regent's Road](../narrative/game_01/CH01_MQ03.md) and the
[Chapter 1 closing authorization](../narrative/game_01/CHAPTER_01.md).
The [Foundational Lore](../FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md) and
[Revelation Plan](../narrative/saga/REVELATION_PLAN.md) retain authority over
hidden history, cosmology and Game I's disclosure limits.

## The land under protection

The proposed quarry is near an **old ridge sanctuary outside Eldenmere**.
This is a separate site from the village sanctuary where Serene, Caelan and
Tomas live. The dispute does not concern quarrying underneath their home.

Marked boundaries enclose the old sanctuary's reserve. The reserve includes
the sanctuary buildings, a wooded hill and buried foundations extending beyond
the visible walls. Protection applies to the ground beneath the reserve as
well as its surface.

Aldric's proposed stone seam lies inside that boundary, although the proposed
work is clear of the standing walls. Neither party disputes that location.
Their disagreement concerns whether excavation should be authorized within
the reserve on the evidence and needs they have now.

The ridge sanctuary is **not established as the original temple of the Great
Sacrifice**. Do not identify the two sites or invent a confirmed connection
between them. The old sanctuary's complete history and buried layout remain
unknown to the people involved.

## Sacred keeping and public responsibility

Local faith understands life as received from the gods and returning to them.
The sanctuary ground is held in keeping across generations, rather than
treated as a resource belonging solely to its present custodian. This is a
religious understanding held sincerely by the community; it does not disclose
the full historical or metaphysical truth behind that belief.

The covenant gives that keeping a recognized public form. Civil authorities
recognize the reserve and the custodians' duties toward it. Its protection is
therefore more than a personal objection by Serene or a claim that the order
may do whatever it wishes with its land.

Civil authorities, sanctuary keepers and the surrounding community share
responsibility for that keeping. The covenant is a custodial trust, not the
order's unrestricted ownership of the reserve.

Custody also carries service obligations. Preserving the site and its records
belongs alongside care, food, practical assistance and responsibility toward
the surrounding community. The people needing the road are among those the
custodians serve. Protection does not release Serene from helping them.

The covenant's exact founding date, the people who first agreed it, its formal
name and its complete institutional history remain open. No new religious
hierarchy or detailed civil constitution is established here.

## Uses, limits and permission

The reserve remains usable land. Its protected status does not forbid every
footstep, tool or ordinary act of maintenance.

| Activity | Established position |
| --- | --- |
| Existing paths and ordinary passage | Permitted within the reserve's established limits |
| Grazing and woodland care | Permitted within those limits |
| Ordinary repairs | Permitted within established maintenance limits; not a general license to open buried works |
| Surface inspection and surveying | Permitted without opening the ground or disturbing buried construction |
| Opening a quarry face or driving a shaft | Restricted; requires joint review and exceptional authorization |
| Opening or disturbing buried works | Restricted; requires joint review and exceptional authorization |
| Moving or surrendering the protected boundary | Beyond the local custodian's authority |

Serene can cooperate with surface inspection and ordinary work within her
custodial responsibilities. She cannot personally sell away the protection,
redraw the boundary or turn a new quarry into ordinary maintenance.

Joint review brings the custodians' records and knowledge together with the
surveyors' and engineers' assessment. It is a substantive examination of the
proposed work, rather than a requirement that the government accept a claim
without being allowed to investigate it.

The chapter-ending formal authorization remains a later escalation. This
guide does not invent an emergency override clause, establish the full permit
procedure or settle every legal question about that authorization. MQ03 ends
without permission to excavate.

## The public evidence behind Serene's caution

Serene's position rests on evidence she can share with Aldric. It does not
depend on asking him to trust an undisclosed prediction of catastrophe.

The public basis includes:

- An old plan showing foundations continuing beneath the wooded hill, beyond
  the standing sanctuary walls.
- An old account of workers injured by an unexplained discharge when they
  opened a passage. The passage was closed afterward.

The account concerns a **small, local historical event**. It is distinct from
the recent lightning surges affecting the region. Its existence does not
prove that those events share a cause.

The plan and account have been shared with Aldric. He is not dismissing
evidence he has been forbidden to see. Both parties know there is buried work
whose extent and purpose are incompletely understood, and a recorded injury
associated with opening a passage.

Those records justify investigation and caution. They do not establish that
every shallow cut is dangerous, explain the discharge's mechanism or prove
that a new quarry would cause a catastrophe. No curse or divine punishment is
confirmed. The exact extent of the buried works remains uncertain.

## The recent road damage and Aldric's request

Before the game's opening, an extraordinarily powerful lightning surge
destroyed **Eldenmere's road south to the next town**. A handful of similar
events have occurred elsewhere in the region. Aldric has already visited a
couple of affected communities and helped with relief.

This is early foreshadowing of disturbed aether flow. It is not an established
widespread cataclysm, and the people in Chapter 1 do not know its deeper
mechanism. The old passage injury is not another newly occurring road strike.

Repair work across the affected communities has strained available stone,
transport and repair resources. Aldric seeks the nearer seam because its
material could serve several towns. His request is a response to existing
damage and relief needs, rather than expansion pursued solely for prestige.

More distant stone remains a real alternative. Its longer haul uses carts,
money and time that other repairs also need. The conflict does not require
every other quarry or route to be unusable.

## Serene's immediate contribution

Serene offers useful help while withholding her endorsement of excavation:

- Cut stone already set aside for courtyard repairs.
- Meals for the road crew.
- Immediate joint surface inspection of the proposed site.
- A request for further records to support the joint review.

The stone is an existing supply, not permission to extract replacement stone
from the reserve. Giving it up defers the sanctuary's own courtyard work.
Serene accepts a concrete cost and participates in relief rather than merely
asking Aldric to wait.

Her offer helps the immediate work but does not provide the stone volume
needed across several towns. Meals and inspection likewise cannot substitute
for construction material. Aldric can value the contribution and still find
the remaining shortage serious.

## Why the disagreement remains unresolved

Aldric proposes a **shallow, supervised trial cut**, with work stopped if
masonry appears. This is a limited proposal under his understanding of the
risk. He is not knowingly accepting a lethal outcome or planning unrestricted
digging through a discovered structure.

Serene cannot establish that stopping when masonry becomes visible would
avoid disturbing the buried works. The plan does not show their complete
extent, and the old injury does not explain what caused the discharge. She
therefore wants review before an irreversible cut inside the reserve.

They can agree on immediate relief, surface inspection and obtaining records
without agreeing on excavation. Surface inspection supplies useful evidence
but cannot instantly resolve the underground uncertainties. Further hauling
can supply stone, but prolongs the costs Aldric is trying to reduce.

The competing responsibilities are real: Aldric must restore access and
distribute strained resources across damaged communities; Serene must assist
those communities while preserving entrusted ground whose risks remain
incompletely understood. Neither position requires contempt for faith,
engineering or the other person's concern for people.

## Knowledge and disclosure boundaries

The public plan and injury account are separate from Serene's **inherited
warning**, which she privately consults after the chapter-ending authorization.
The player does not read that warning in full. It must not become a complete
answer that Serene could have disclosed to settle the MQ03 disagreement.

Serene does not know the cosmic catastrophe, the full truth of the Great
Sacrifice or the identities of the four surviving Ancients. Neither her faith
nor the public records establish the buried works' full mechanism. Do not
reveal a buried machine or a hidden causal explanation during MQ03.

The broader mechanism, the old site's full history, precise civil procedures,
and later discoveries remain open or concealed under the existing narrative
guides. This approval establishes the local covenant and conflict; it does
not turn those unanswered saga questions into public knowledge.
