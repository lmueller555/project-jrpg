# Auratia World Atlas

**Status: Approved world name, broad geography, regional cultural foundations
and opening regional context.**

**Implementation:** The recent surge damage and covenant context below are
approved documentation; runtime and map-art integration remain pending.

**Auratia** contains four major continents, a significant central island chain
and a northern circumpolar ocean region.
This atlas places Eldenmere within that wider landscape and gives future
stories places, livelihoods and cultural relationships to draw upon. Exact
borders and distances remain open; illustrative encounters are story seeds,
not committed quests.

The existing village, characters, quests and local geography retain their
approved facts. [Foundational Lore](../FOUNDATIONAL_LORE.md) governs the
world's aether, ancient history and hidden cosmology. The
[Revelation Plan](../narrative/saga/REVELATION_PLAN.md) governs what characters
and players may learn. The atlas is a writer's reference; a character's own
map or understanding can be narrower.

## The world's name

**Auratia** is the world's name. Its language of origin, ancient etymology,
historical forms and regional pronunciations remain to be developed. The name
does not by itself reveal the world's hidden history.

## The broad arrangement

Merovar, Serevin and Othara surround the **Amber Sea**, a warm central sea with
major connections to the wider oceans. The **Rillan Isles** lie along several
routes across it. Farther south, the **Southern Ocean** separates Othara from
the polar continent **Istrava**. In the north, the **Nivren Crown** includes
the polar ocean and the far northern shores of Merovar and Serevin; it is not
another continent. This arrangement makes contact between societies plausible
while leaving voyages expensive, seasonal and consequential.

| Land | Relative position | Geographic range | Initial story use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Merovar** | Northwest of the Amber Sea | Temperate valleys, wooded uplands, alpine country, river plains, marshes, southern coasts and a drier eastern interior; colder forests and tundra farther north | Eldenmere and the first game's regional setting |
| **Serevin** | East and northeast of the Amber Sea | Northern continental steppe, large river basins, seasonal monsoon country, forested uplands and maritime lowlands | Distant connections, visitors and possible later journeys |
| **Othara** | South of the Amber Sea | A wet eastern coast, cool elevated plateaus, savanna transitions and an arid leeward interior | Trading relationships and substantial space for future games |
| **Rillan Isles** | Island chain within the central sea routes | Sheltered harbours, wet volcanic highlands, cultivated slopes, reefs and exposed outer islands | Maritime communities with connections to the three surrounding continents |
| **Nivren Crown** | Northern polar ocean and the far northern margins of Merovar and Serevin | Central pack ice, fjords, tundra, coastal islands and rocky offshore skerries | Coastal households, seasonal herding, cold-water trade and rescue obligations |
| **Istrava** | Southern polar continent across the Southern Ocean from Othara | Interior ice plateau, glacier coasts and a subpolar volcanic island fringe | Small island communities and rotating shore stations; substantial room for later stories |

The atlas does not settle the planet's total land area, exact distances,
coastline shapes or the existence of additional landmasses beyond those named
here. Large interiors, remote coasts and the detailed extent of polar surveys
remain available for later development. The original sacrificial temple has
no assigned location here.

## Where Eldenmere belongs

Eldenmere's geographic hierarchy is:

**Auratia → Merovar → upper River Leth watershed → Elden Vale → Eldenmere.**

Elden Vale occupies wooded foothills on the wetter western side of the
**Greyfold Mountains**. Eldenmere is an inland village within it. The existing
local map has northern headwater woodland, terraced ground, a descending
north–south brook, a raised sanctuary and a working mill. It remains a
brook-side settlement; the name does not establish a lake or coastal location.

At the larger scale, the village brook joins other tributaries that
eventually feed the **River Leth**. The Leth runs south through fertile
riverlands and reaches the Amber Sea through the **Rookfen** delta. Exact
tributary junctions are left for a regional map.

The village's controlled millrace and irrigation supplies remain distinct
from the main brook. The short wheel-feeding branch runs east–west locally.
The blockage in MQ02 obstructs the mill supply; it does not stop the whole
river or watershed. See the approved
[Eldenmere elevation and water contract](eldenmere/blockout/README.md).

Regent Aldric's established authority is regional. His
administration's immediate concerns lie in Elden Vale and neighbouring upper
Leth road and quarry districts. His title's constitutional basis, any higher
sovereign authority and exact political boundaries remain open. A geographic
region in the atlas is not automatically a nation or province.

### The region at the opening of Game I

Before the opening, an extraordinarily powerful lightning surge destroyed
the road from Eldenmere to the next town south. That town has no approved
name. A handful of similar events elsewhere in the region have led Aldric to
visit a couple of affected communities and provide relief. Repairs strain
available stone, transport and labour across those communities; this is not a
widespread cataclysm. See [Regional Surges](../narrative/game_01/REGIONAL_SURGES.md)
for the recent incidents and arrival context.

The resulting material need drives Aldric's request to quarry a nearer seam
inside the protected reserve of an old ridge sanctuary near Eldenmere. This
is a separate site from the inhabited village sanctuary. Its marked reserve
covers a wooded hill and buried foundations as well as sanctuary buildings.
The [Sanctuary Covenant](SANCTUARY_COVENANT.md) records the shared custodial
trust, public evidence and relief obligations behind the disagreement;
neither the site's complete history nor the surges' deeper cause is public
knowledge.

The detailed regional setting is in [Elden Vale](regions/ELDEN_VALE.md).

## Merovar and the first game's neighbourhood

The Greyfold range creates a useful geographic contrast: moist western
weather supports Elden Vale's woodland and pasture, while the eastern
rain-shadow country becomes drier. Elevation provides conifer slopes,
moorland and alpine meadows within a regional journey. The southern river
plain and delta have different livelihoods and concerns from the uplands.

| Region | Landscape | Everyday cultural foundations | A material source of tension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Elden Vale** | Temperate woods, streams, terraces and pasture | Sanctuary care, household crafts, milling and shared maintenance work | Useful improvements can conflict with protected ground and local obligations |
| **Greyfold Uplands** | Conifer slopes, moorland, alpine meadows and mountain passes | Herding, quarrying, pass maintenance and hospitality along difficult routes | Quarry access, grazing rights and maintaining safe passes |
| **Lower Leth** | Fertile river plains and seasonal floodlands | Grain markets, water-sharing agreements, tenant farms and market towns | Upstream and downstream interests diverge over water and flood protection |
| **Rookfen** | Freshwater marshes becoming a brackish delta | Reed work, fishing, ferry services and homes adapted to wet ground | Draining land creates farms while threatening existing livelihoods |
| **Marris Coast** | Southern cliffs, estuaries and sheltered Amber Sea harbours | Ship repair, salt production, migrant neighbourhoods and merchant institutions | Transit fees, access to waterfront work and the price of imported necessities |
| **Redstone March** | Drier grassland and scrub east of the Greyfold range | Pastoral routes, quarry settlements, market centres and well maintenance | Town boundaries and private holdings can interrupt shared access to water |

Game I's core remains Elden Vale and its nearby upland and upper-river
connections. These regions offer choices for later story planning; they do
not require the first game to visit every biome or reach the coast. The
southern delta, farther east and the rest of Merovar can remain outside its
playable route.

Read the continental detail in [Merovar](continents/MEROVAR.md).

## Other continents, the island chain and the poles

**Serevin** gives us permanent river cities, mobile and settled communities
in the northern steppe, upland settlements and busy coastal ports. Its
regions are **Talven Steppe**, **Vashen Basin** and **Namar Coast**.
Seasonal water and long travel routes matter in different ways to farmers,
herders, public officials, artisans and traders. Their interests can conflict
within the same community. See [Serevin](continents/SEREVIN.md).

**Othara** has a strong contrast between the **Velin Raincoast**, the elevated
**Ossan Plateau** and the leeward **Damar Drylands**. Forest and coastal
settlements, plateau towns, farming districts and dryland routes depend upon
one another. Differences in water access, land tenure and transport create
cultural histories worth developing. See [Othara](continents/OTHARA.md).

**Rillan** is an archipelago, not a continent or a single island nation.
The **Harbor Chain**, **Rainward Isles** and **Outer Reaches** have different
relationships with trade and the sea. Port councils, farming communities,
boatbuilding families and lighthouse crews do not benefit equally from the
same shipping routes. See [Rillan Isles](continents/RILLAN_ISLES.md).

**Nivren Crown** encompasses the **Pale Sea**, the **Rimeward Shores** and the
**Skeld Isles**. The North Pole lies over water beneath the Pale Sea's drifting
pack ice. Long-established coastal settlements and mobile herding households
occupy the region's continental margins and islands; their lives include
fishing, boatbuilding, trade, teaching and the maintenance of rescue stores.

**Istrava** contains the **White Expanse**, **Bluewall Coast** and **Cinder
Isles**. The South Pole lies on its interior ice plateau. Small island
communities, including families with generations of residence, differ from
the rotating crews at mainland shore stations. Fisheries, imported supplies
and local geothermal sites support limited settlement; the deep interior is
sparsely surveyed. See [Polar Regions](POLAR_REGIONS.md) for both poles.

These areas can first become familiar through people, goods and stories:
an imported tool, a visiting performer, a sailor's household, a healer trained
elsewhere or a disagreement over a familiar religious phrase. An early
encounter need not promise a later visit.

## Connections that make the world feel inhabited

| Connection | Goods and work | People and ideas travelling with them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elden Vale and the upper Leth | Timber, wool, milled flour, stone, tools and repair work | Sanctuary workers, herders, seasonal labourers and road crews |
| Lower Leth, Rookfen and Marris | Grain, river transport, reeds, fish, salt and coastal shipping | Bargemen, tenant families, apprentices and merchants |
| Merovar and Rillan | Merovar timber, wool and grain; island produce, navigation and ship services | Migrant households, crews, travelling teachers and performers |
| Serevin and the Amber Sea ports | Basin cloth, paper and preserved produce; steppe wool and livestock; imported metals, salt and timber | Clerks, scholars, transport workers, traders and mixed-language families |
| Othara and the island routes | Plateau ores and woven goods, dryland salts, coastal crops and forest products | Metalworkers, water engineers, healers, students and migrant crews |
| Nivren Crown and the northern continental coasts | Fish, boat work and coastal passage knowledge; imported grain, tools, cloth and timber where needed | Fishing households, herders, teachers, traders and rescue crews |
| Othara and Istrava across the Southern Ocean | Fisheries and repair work; imported grain, timber, fuel and equipment | Island families, navigators, healers, surveyors and rotating station crews |

These are broad foundations, not fixed inventories or a complete trade
simulation. Each region needs food producers and household work as well as its
most visible exports. Ports should contain settled families and ordinary
services; upland communities should contain skilled crafts and intellectual
life. Travel permits, tolls, refuge and access to work are possible story
pressures rather than universally identical laws.

## Faith, magicks and cultural variation

Eldenmere's established Festival of Light combines remembrance with living
community. Other places may express related beliefs through different
gatherings, household practices or public observances. These practices can
share historical fragments without establishing one identical religion
across the world. The names, hierarchy and relationships of major traditions
remain to be chosen.

The continental documents describe such practices as communal meals, repaired
lamps, remembrance records or household acknowledgements. Their theological
claims remain beliefs. They do not settle souls, reincarnation or the
consciousness of the dormant Ancients. The Festival of Light's season and the
world's calendar also remain open.

Magicks obey the same established conservation limits everywhere. Regional
differences can arise through teaching, tools, occupations, access to training
and institutions. Geography and ancestry do not automatically determine a
person's magical strength. Each region can contain healers, offensive casters,
craft workers, people with modest abilities and people whose work uses little
magicks.

## Turning the atlas into scenes

For a region's first substantial appearance, develop a household, a place of
work and a public institution before writing a lore lecture. Give residents
different concerns: a ferry owner and a reed grower can disagree about the
same embankment while both care about their home. Let a traveller's mistaken
expectation meet a specific person rather than a whole population confirming
the stereotype.

The approved [companion foundations](../narrative/game_01/COMPANIONS.md) place
Lucan Rooke in the Redstone March, Rhea Sorel on the wooded western Greyfold
slopes and Edran Hale in the Lower Leth. Specific home settlements remain open.
Use the atlas to develop future backgrounds as the characters' needs become
clear. Existing identities and relationships retain their approved facts.

The world name, continental arrangement, Eldenmere's placement and regional
foundations are approved. More precise borders, languages, political systems,
religious institutions, distances and calendar details remain open and can
grow from the stories that need them.
